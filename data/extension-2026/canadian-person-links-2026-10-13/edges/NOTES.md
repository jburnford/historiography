**Thompson.** Both 1981 Labour/Le Travailleur essays were read in full on Érudit. Kealey
(pp. 82–83, 87) calls Thompson's influence "our concern" and accepts that British insights
must be shown to apply "to the Canadian context". Bercuson (pp. 97–98, 108) says Canadian new
labour historians "follow the thinking of E.P. Thompson" and objects that his concepts "do not
necessarily apply anywhere else". This upgrades `wcreq_kealey_1981` and `wcreq_bercuson_1981`
from abstract to page level and confirms Kealey's end page (94). **Overlap:** `edge_886`
(marx → cdn_working_class) already carries Thompson's name in its label and map label. If this
edge is accepted, relabel `edge_886` as Marxist class analysis.

**Scott.** Valverde's 1990 Labour/Le Travail review essay on Scott and Riley was read in full.
It sets Scott in an English-Canadian discourse debate and says she "deserves to be carefully read".
With Pierson's 1991 opening (already checked), these are two pre-2000 Canadian texts read at
the relevant passages. Linda Kealey (1992), Pierson (1992) and Parr (1995) cite Scott at the
reference-list level only. The edge complements `edge_840`; it does not duplicate it.

**Hall.** No checked pre-2000 passage was found in which a Canadian women's or gender historian
engages Family Fortunes. The candidates are Darroch 1999, whose Crossref references include
Family Fortunes and whose abstract qualifies the "domestic revolution" thesis for Ontario (family
history, not this field), and Parr's 1993 CHR review of Hall and Morgan's 1998 Histoire sociale
review of Davidoff (metadata only; bot checks blocked both). Morgan 1996 works the same
terrain, but nothing checked links it to Davidoff and Hall. The strong PhD pairing
(58/108, lift 2.13) probably reflects post-2000 uptake: Perry 2001, Hall 2002 and
Households of Faith 2002. McKay's 2000 prospectus (OpenAlex references) cites neither Hall
nor Davidoff.

**To unlock the Hall edge:** read Parr 1993 (CHR 74:598–99, Project MUSE), Morgan 1998 (Histoire
sociale 31:61), or the introduction to Morgan 1996 or Gendered Pasts (1999) through library access.

**Access.** Project MUSE, the York journal sites, De Gruyter and H-Net blocked automated reading,
and Érudit did so intermittently. OpenAlex reference lists are matching metadata, not readings.
