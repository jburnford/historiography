# Prominent historians missing from the atlas: candidate audit (2026-10-17)

The user asked for this audit after finding Robert Brenner absent ("Brenner needs his own entry as his debate with Pomeranz is minor compared with the Brenner debate"). The question: are other major figures missing altogether, not just thinly drawn?

**Method.** Two offline, name-level signals, checked against the atlas's people (surname plus first initial):
1. **Canadian PhD citations.** Citing dissertations among the 2,037 Canadian History PhD bibliographies (`data/field-research/dissertation-citations-2026-10-11/cited-people.csv`). Canada-weighted.
2. **Books reviewed.** Distinct books reviewed across H-Net, Reviews in History and JSTOR-era review records (registry v12). US-weighted, older, and favouring prolific book authors.

**Limit.** Neither signal measures historiographical importance. Brenner, central to the field, scores only 13 citing PhDs and 4 reviewed books: the Brenner debate was argued in articles and one edited volume. These lists generate candidates; they do not rank them. Under the person-entry principle, framework thinkers get entries and content historians stay on rosters.
