## Why groups first
The atlas teaches historiography in Canada, but it has no field for Canadian historiography.
The user decided (2026-10-12) to create the Canadian group entries first and then attach the
person entries to them. The Canadian historians in
`../canadian-historiography-scholars-request-2026-10-12/` each have exactly one primary
placement: in a group below, or, for Canadian environmental historians, in the existing
Environmental history entry. The atlas already treats national schools as entries (Annales,
the US Progressives, History Workshop, Bielefeld); these follow that pattern.

## Each proposed entry

### Laurentian school & the national tradition (`cdn_national_tradition`)
Staples and Laurentian interpretations (Innis, Creighton), metropolitanism (Careless), and the political-national synthesis of the Centenary Series; the tradition Berger's Writing of Canadian History (1976) analyses.
- **Period:** 1920s–1960s, with later reassessment.
- **Related atlas entries:** nationaltradition, economic, political, progressive
- **Primary people:** Innis, Harold A, Creighton, Donald, Careless, J.M.S, Brown, Robert Craig
- **Also anchoring:** Berger, Carl, Morton, W.L, Stanley, George F.G, Cook, Ramsay, Stacey, C.P
- **Most-cited works (citing PhDs, 2009–26):** The Sense of Power: Studies in the Ideas of Canadian Imperialism, 1867-1914 (1970; 78); The Fur Trade in Canada: An Introduction to Canadian Economic History (1930; 48); The Writing of Canadian History: Aspects of English-Canadian Historical Writing since 1900 (1986; 48); The Regenerators: Social Criticism in Late Victorian English Canada (1985; 41); Canada and the Age of Conflict (1981; 34)

### English-Canadian intellectual & cultural history (`cdn_intellectual_cultural`)
Ideas of nation, empire and reform (Berger, Cook), the intellectuals of the state (Owram), antimodernism and the invented folk (McKay), pageantry, myth and memory (Nelles, Francis, Vance, Coates).
- **Period:** 1970s onward.
- **Related atlas entries:** intellectualhistory, memory, nationalismstudies, culture
- **Primary people:** Berger, Carl, Owram, Doug, Radforth, Ian, Francis, Daniel, Vance, Jonathan, Coates, Colin M, Heaman, Elsbeth, Cook, Ramsay

- **Most-cited works (citing PhDs, 2009–26):** The Liberal Order Framework: A Prospectus for a Reconnaissance of Canadian History (2000; 90); The Sense of Power: Studies in the Ideas of Canadian Imperialism, 1867-1914 (1970; 78); Born at the Right Time: A History of the Baby Boom Generation (1996; 70); Death So Noble: Memory, Meaning, and the First World War (1997; 67); The Other Quiet Revolution: National Identities in English Canada, 1945-71 (2006; 60)

### Religion, social reform & secularization in Canada (`cdn_religion_reform`)

### Liberal order framework (`liberal_order_framework`)
McKay's prospectus (2000) for reading Canada as a liberal project of rule, closer to a history of capitalism than to moral regulation: the spread of a liberal order of property, individualism and market relations, its state-formation roots (Greer and Radforth 1992, Curtis 2001), and its debate (Constant and Ducharme 2009).
- **Period:** 1990s–2010s.
- **Related atlas entries:** karl, economic, political, weber, none:history_of_capitalism (no atlas entry)

### The 'Who Killed Canadian History?' debate (`who_killed_debate`, debate)
The 1990s quarrel over national history: Bliss's 'Privatizing the Mind' (1991) and Granatstein's Who Killed Canadian History? (1998) charged social historians with fragmenting the national story; responses defended social, gender, labour and regional history (Kealey 1992, McKillop 1999, and later Dummitt and Dawson's Contesting Clio's Craft, 2009). Its roots lie in the 'limited identities' argument (Careless 1969; Buckner 2000).
- **Period:** 1991–2000s.
- **Related atlas entries:** revival, truthdebate, nationaltradition, social, cdn_political_national (proposed), cdn_regional (proposed)
- **Primary people:** Bliss, Michael
- **Also anchoring:** Granatstein, J.L, Kealey, Gregory S, Owram, Doug, Careless, J.M.S, Buckner, Phillip
