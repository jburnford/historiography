# Q54: an Indigenous critique of native–newcomer historiography before 2000

Researched 2026-10-12 for `native_newcomer`. Nothing has been applied to the drafts or the atlas. The structured version, with every URL and verification status, is in `q54-indigenous-critique.json`. The counts come from `q54_counts.py`.

## Answer

**Yes, there is one.** The clearest checked case is Winona Stevenson's essay, published under that name; she is now Winona Wheeler. I read it in full: "Indigenous Voices, Indigenous Histories, Part I: The Othering of Indigenous History", *Saskatchewan History* 50, no. 2 (Fall 1998): 24–27. It is open access on iPortal ([record](https://iportal.usask.ca/node/68012), [issue PDF](https://iportal.usask.ca/sites/iportal.usask.ca/files/content/68012/SKHistory_Vol50_No2.pdf)). The scan has no text layer, so I ran OCR on pp. 24–27.

- It addresses "Historians of Indian–White Relations". It argues that the concept "presupposes at least two parties". By "focusing on the historical remnants left by one side of that relationship, historians reinforce the colonialist notion that Western historical canons and conventions are superior" (pp. 24–25).
- It credits the recent "outpouring" of relations history and work celebrating Indigenous peoples "as active agents in encounter with Europeans". Its notes cite Carter, Pettipas, Tough, Ray and Peers.
- It then argues that "their continued dependence on documents maintains the academic tradition of telling the stories of my peoples' historical experiences within the colonialist framework" (p. 25).
- The author note says she is Cree and a member of Fisher River Cree First Nation, and Head of Indian Studies at the Saskatchewan Indian Federated College.
- The date is **Fall 1998**, not 1999.

**The second checked case is LaRocque's 1999 Manitoba dissertation.** It is already an atlas source, but only p. 34 had been read. The passages now read (page numbers are PDF pages of `NQ45006.pdf`):

- PDF p. 249: criticises "the neo cross-cultural approach adopted by Native specialist scholars from about the 1960s–1980s era (Bailey, Jaenen, Trigger, Brown, Van Kirk)". She grants that it is "much improved" on earlier racist work, but says its ethnological description results "in Othering the very people the researcher is trying to make understandable".
- PDF pp. 102 and 109: fur-trade historians' double standard, such as the Indians' "covetous disposition", and E.E. Rich's "crafty".
- PDF p. 30, note: Trigger's "The Historians' Indian" is "an astonishingly optimistic view of French historiography".
- PDF p. 135: Trigger's foreword to Sioui is "a typical example of containment".

## Recommendation

1. **Cite Stevenson (Wheeler) 1998** as the pre-2000 Canadian Indigenous critique of the relations framing.
   - Use a new source `nnreq_stevenson_1998`, replacing `nnreq_wheeler_unverified`.
   - Reuse the atlas person `winona_wheeler`, labelled "Winona Wheeler (published as Winona Stevenson)".
   - Add her to the critics strand as a **critic**, not a member, alongside Sioui and LaRocque.
2. **Strengthen the LaRocque 1999 claim** with the pages read above.
3. **Keep Sioui** as already drafted.
4. **Do not present Adams as a critic of this field.** At most, call *Prison of Grass* an earlier counter-history of official and school history.
5. **Keep Dickason as a member of the field.**
6. **Keep Trigger 1986 and Brownlie & Kelm 1994 (with the 1995 replies) as non-Indigenous context.**

Suggested sentence for the description, replacing "Indigenous scholars proposed other starting points…":

> Indigenous scholars questioned the framing itself. Sioui (1989) argued that conventional history had no method for Indigenous self-interpretation. Winona Stevenson (Wheeler) (1998) argued that historians of 'Indian–White relations', relying on the records of one side, kept even agency-centred work within a colonialist framework and set oral history aside. LaRocque (1999) found that the cross-cultural ethnohistory of Trigger, Brown and Van Kirk still distanced and 'othered' the people it studied.

Cautions:

- Date the essay Fall 1998.
- Don't claim it was "the first".
- Attribute identity only to the author note in the source.
- Don't cite Part 3 or the 2000 dissertation until they are checked.
- Keep Brownlie and Kelm phrased as a question, because the body of their article has not been read.
