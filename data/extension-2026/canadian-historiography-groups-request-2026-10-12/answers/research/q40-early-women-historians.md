**Francophone counterpart (verified table of contents):** Louise Bienvenue and François-Olivier Dorais, eds., *Profession historienne? Femmes et pratique de l'histoire au Canada français, XIXe–XXe siècles* (PUL, 2023), doi:10.1515/9782763759951. Its chapters cover Laure Conan, Marie-Claire Daveluy and Maria Mondoux, Marguerite Michaud, Corinne Rocheleau-Rouleau, women in the RHAF, and later academics.

Bienvenue's article "Marie-Claire Daveluy (1880–1968), historienne des femmes", *Histoire sociale* 51:104 (2018), doi:10.1353/his.2018.0029, has an abstract I read. It says Daveluy insisted on women's role in French-Canadian history.

## How to frame them without overclaiming
- Call them "earlier women writers of history," not founders of women's history.
- Most of them wrote national, local, fur-trade or political history. Only two are documented as foregrounding women:
  - Skelton: Coulter's review of Crowley says she "sought to write women back into Canadian history."
  - Daveluy: Bienvenue (2018).
- Present the group through the 1997 recovery and its question about professionalisation and masculinisation. Keep the editors' Anglo-Celtic caveat.
- Neatby and Ormsby were the first and second women presidents of the CHA (see `cha-women-presidents.md`). They illustrate women in the profession, not the field itself.
- Daveluy and *Profession historienne?* are post-2000 scholarship. Use them as evidence or as `extension_coverage`, not as period milestones.

**Possible sentence for the draft:** "Women wrote Canadian history long before the field existed: as popular, local and regional historians (Machar, Skinner, Clark Wright), as independent scholars such as Isabel Skelton, and as a few university historians (Neatby, Ormsby); in French Canada Marie-Claire Daveluy foregrounded women in the history of New France. Boutilier and Prentice's *Creating Historical Memory* (1997) recovered this work and asked how professionalisation had masculinised history."

## Caveats
- The chapters themselves are unread.
- Laval is thin in the citation corpus (9 PhDs).
- Earlier writers are often cited as primary sources, not as historiography.
