# Q81: Francophone voices in the "Who Killed Canadian History?" debate

Researched 2026-10-12. This is an answer for review; nothing in the drafts or the atlas has been changed. Full records, URLs and
the citing PhDs are in `q81-who-killed-francophone.json`.

## Answer in brief

**Did francophones engage? Yes, with Granatstein, but not with Bliss.** They answered as reviewers, in French-language
journals and the CHA *Bulletin*, in 1998–99:

| Response | Where | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Félix Bouvier, review | *BHP* 7:2 (1999): 156–58, doi:10.7202/1060332ar | metadata; snippets |
| Nadia Fahmy-Eid, review | *BHP* 7:3 (1999): 145–49, doi:10.7202/1060359ar | metadata; snippet ("tendentious") |
| Jack Jedwab and Grégory Slogar, review | *BHP* 7:3 (1999): 150–53, doi:10.7202/1060360ar | metadata |
| Alfred Dubuc, "À propos de : Qui a tué l'histoire du Canada ?" | *BHP* 8:1 (1999): 195ff., doi:10.7202/1060400ar | first page (OpenAlex) |
| Micheline Dumont, review | CHA *Bulletin* 25:1 (1999): 12–14, depot.erudit.org/id/005320dd | **full text read** |

- **Dumont's review** is the strongest checked voice:
  - She says Granatstein's facts on Quebec schooling are "erronées". The "killers", she writes, are "les acteurs d'une
    conjoncture inédite", not assassins.
  - She calls his "réquisitoire ... féroce contre les praticiens de l'histoire sociale" and says he dismisses any history
    that does not aim at Canadian unity: "On ne trahit pas plus clairement des visées politiques."
  - She criticizes his military emphasis from a gender perspective.
  - She joins the two debates herself. She cites Lucia Ferretti's chapter in Comeau and Dionne, *À propos de l'histoire
    nationale* (1998), and Létourneau's surveys of students.
  - She records that the book "planait" over McGill's bilingual colloquium "Giving the Past a Future / L'Avenir de notre
    passé" (January 1999).
- **Dubuc** reads the book as mourning two deaths: national history and "l'histoire politique comme discipline". That is
  the *BHP*'s own cause.
- **Bliss (1991–92).** No francophone reply was found. Every respondent in the *JCS* (1992) is anglophone. The 1997 *BHP*
  editorial "Vers une privatisation de l'histoire?" looks like an echo of Bliss's title, but it is about purging judicial
  records.
- **Létourneau's 2000 *CHR* essay** answers the debate's question: neither a "unitariste" nor an "atomisant" narrative,
  but "canadianité". Its abstract names neither Granatstein nor Bliss, and the full text is paywalled (`not_verified`).
  All eight forum respondents were anglophone.
- **Fecteau 1999** is about Rudin and Quebec "revisionism", not *Who Killed*. It is the wrong evidence for a francophone
  voice in this debate.

**Was there a parallel Quebec debate? Yes, a separate one.**
- **1992–97.** The *BHP* (first issue 1992) campaigned for political history: Bellavance 1992, Sarra-Bournet 1995.
  Bélanger's "Pour un retour à l'histoire politique" followed (*RHAF* 51:2, 1997). Bélanger blames Annales and the new
  social history, and does not refer to Bliss.
- **1998–2000.** Comeau and Dionne, eds., 1998; Létourneau 2000.
- **2006–07.** The controversy over the secondary programme began with Antoine Robitaille's *Le Devoir* article of 27 April 2006 (see
  Dagenais and Laville, *RHAF* 2007; Bouvier's 2008 *bilan* for the Bouchard–Taylor commission).
- **2007–12.** Bock-Côté 2007; Petitclerc's defence of social history (2009); Bédard and D'Arcy's *L'histoire nationale
  négligée* (Fondation Lionel-Groulx, 2011); Bouvier et al. 2012.

The structure is the same as in English Canada: political and national historians charge social history with
denationalizing; social historians reply. The nation, however, is Quebec's. English Canada appears only as a foil:
multiculturalism "religion d'État", the Historica foundation's "nation building". Bouvier's 2008 *bilan* (full text
searched) never mentions Granatstein, Bliss or *Who Killed*.

**Did the two debates engage each other?** Only one way, and through people rather than texts. Francophones reviewed
Granatstein, and two of the reviewers belonged to the Quebec debate:
- Dionne co-edited *À propos de l'histoire nationale*.
- Bouvier later wrote the 2008 *bilan* and co-edited the 2012 volume.

Dumont and Létourneau linked the two in print. Anglophones met the Quebec side only in Létourneau's 2000 forum.

## Recommendation: add francophone respondents to `who_killed_debate`

Francophones did answer Granatstein, so the entry should show it. The Quebec debate is a related but separate quarrel. The
two debates' texts did not exchange, and most of the Quebec debate falls after 2000, outside this entry's coverage. A merged
strand or a joint entry would therefore overstate the connection.

Concretely:
1. **Dumont.** Add Micheline Dumont as a critic. She is already on the `quebec_historiography` and `cdn_women_gender`
   rosters, and the source is the full text of her review.
2. **Dionne and Fahmy-Eid.** Add them as title-level critics, marked `needs_review`. Bouvier, Jedwab and Slogar, and
   Dubuc go in the sources and description only.
3. **Scope note.** Revise "largely English-Canadian": francophones answered in *RHAF*, the *BHP* and the CHA *Bulletin*,
   while Quebec's own debate ran separately.
4. **Comparison edge `cdng_wk_rel_07`.** Keep it as drafted, but re-ground it on Dumont 1999 instead of Fecteau 1999.
   Fecteau belongs only to `quebec_historiography`.
5. **Later.** If coverage ever extends past 2000, the 2006–2012 Quebec debate could become its own Debate entry.
