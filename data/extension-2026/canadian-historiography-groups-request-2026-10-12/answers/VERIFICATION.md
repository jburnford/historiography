# Verification of open questions (Canadian historiography groups, 2026-10-12)

`verification.json` holds the machine-readable record: one object per question, with `answer`, `status`, `evidence` (URL plus a short quote or note) and `implication_for_draft`. This file summarises it.

**Method.** Sources were Crossref, OpenAlex, publisher and journal pages, Érudit and journal PDFs (text extracted locally), scholarly reviews, author profiles, and the Wikidata MCP for Q21. Some sources could only be seen as search-result snippets, because Project MUSE, JSTOR, the York journals and some Érudit pages returned bot checks. The evidence notes in the JSON say where this happened. Nothing is answered from memory alone.

**Status counts:** 11 verified, 12 partly verified, 0 not verified. Within the partly verified answers, some sub-claims remain unchecked (listed under each item and in the closing list).

## Corrections the drafts need

- **Q79.** The 1992 *Journal of Canadian Studies* pieces answer **Bliss's "Privatizing the Mind" (JCS 26:4, 1991–92)**, not *Who Killed Canadian History?* (1998).
  - G.S. Kealey, "Class in English-Canadian Historical Writing: Neither Privatizing, Nor Sundering," JCS 27:2 (1992): 123–29.
  - L. Kealey, Pierson, Sangster and Strong-Boag, "Teaching Canadian History in the 1990s: Whose 'National' History Are We Lamenting?," JCS 27:2 (1992): 129–31.
  - The 1999 replies to Granatstein are A.B. McKillop, "Who Killed Canadian History? A View from the Trenches," CHR 80:2 (1999): 269–300, and Bryan D. Palmer, "Of Silences and Trenches: A Dissident View of Granatstein's Meaning," **CHR 80:4 (1999): 676–86** (not *Canadian Dimension*).
- **Q55.** Brownlie and Kelm, "Desperately Seeking Absolution: Native Agency as Colonialist Alibi?," CHR 75:4 (Dec. 1994): 543–56.
  - Its targets were agency-centred work on Indian policy and the potlatch law: Cole and Chaikin, *An Iron Hand upon the People*; J.R. Miller's "Owen Glendower, Hotspur, and Canadian Indian Policy"; Tina Loo's "Dan Cranmer's Potlatch." The fur-trade literature was not its subject.
  - The author's U of Manitoba profile reads "Jarvis Brownlie," pronouns he.
- **Q20.** McKay, CHR 81:4 (2000), doi:10.3138/chr.81.4.616. Cite pp. **617–45**; Crossref's 616–678 is a metadata error.
  - The abstract frames Canada as "a project of liberal rule."
  - "Liberty, equality, property" is Fecteau's paraphrase of McKay, not a checked McKay quotation.
  - The line "Liberal order and capitalism cannot be conflated" comes from McKay's **2009** reply.
- **Q29.** The 1981 critiques are:
  - McNaught, "E.P. Thompson vs Harold Logan," CHR 62:2 (1981): 141–68;
  - Bercuson, "Through the Looking Glass of Culture," *Labour/Le Travailleur* 7 (1981): 95–112;
  - with Kealey's reply in the same issue: "Labour and Working-Class History in Canada: Prospects in the 1980s," L/LT 7 (1981): 67–94.
  - Neither "Labour Historians: Ideology and Practice" nor a Bercuson item in *History and Social Science Teacher* (1981) could be found. Drop both.
- **Q13.** Marshall's 1993 essay is "Canadian Historians, Secularization and the Problem of the Nineteenth Century," CCHA *Historical Studies* 60 (1993–94): 57–81. It defends the secularization thesis against Gauvreau; it is not a general historiographical survey.
