# Political, international & national history (`cdn_political_national`): drafting notes

**Draft status:** proposal for review, 2026-10-12. Nothing in the atlas is changed.

**What it is.** A research field for the Canadian state: parties and mandarins (Granatstein, Whitaker, Owram), war politics and
conscription, external relations (Stacey, Hillmer and Granatstein, Bothwell; Chapnick's critique of the middle-power story),
national syntheses (Bothwell, Drummond and English; Thompson and Seager), political biography in English and French (Brown,
English, Bercuson, Bélanger), business history (Bliss, Drummond), the Cold War security state (Whitaker and Marcuse) and the
British World (Buckner and Francis). Date label: *Granatstein 1975, 1977, 1982 · Whitaker 1977 · Stacey 1977–81 · Bothwell, Drummond
and English 1981 · Owram 1986 · Buckner 1993 · Whitaker and Marcuse 1994 · British World 2006 · coverage through 2000*. Period: period_2.

**Roster:** 16 people. The debate polemics (Granatstein 1998, Bliss 1991) are placed in `who_killed_debate`, per the user's decision.

**Evidence.** 242 of 1,303 strict 2009–2026 PhDs (18.6%) engage the anchors, falling from 23.7% to 10.4%, the steepest fall of my four
drafts. Most-cited: Whitaker and Marcuse (50 PhDs), Owram 1986 (39), Granatstein 1982 (36), Stacey (35), Bothwell 2007 (34).

**Judgement calls.**
- Most of the field's books are verified at catalogue level only. Supporting text was read for Whitaker 1977 (contents), Owram 1986,
  Whitaker and Marcuse 1994, Buckner and Francis 2006 and Chapnick 2005 (descriptions), and Buckner 1993 and Chapnick 2000 (abstract or opening).
- Four relationships: comparison with `political`, with `cdn_national_tradition` (via Stacey and the Centenary Series), with
  `nationaltradition` (British World; the atlas lacks an imperial-history entry), and with `cdn_military` (metadata-level; flagged).
- No women are on the roster; the groups.json anchors include none.

**Questions for the user:** business-history group, Bliss's primary placement, the Stacey/Granatstein overlap with cdn_military,
francophone and women historians, and the British World strand (see `open_questions`).
