# The 'Who Killed Canadian History?' debate (`who_killed_debate`): drafting notes

**Draft status:** proposal for review, 2026-10-12. Nothing in the atlas is changed.

**What it is.** A debate entry: Bliss (1991–92) and Granatstein (1998) charged academic historians with fragmenting the national
story; labour historians (G. Kealey 1992, Palmer 1999), women's historians (L. Kealey, Pierson, Sangster and Strong-Boag 1992;
Strong-Boag 1994) and McKillop (1999) replied; in 2000 Létourneau, Cook, Buckner, McKay and Osborne reassessed; Dummitt and Dawson
(2009) tried to move beyond it. Roots in Cook 1967 and Careless 1969 ("limited identities"). Date label: *Roots: Cook 1967, Careless
1969 · Bliss 1991–92 · replies 1992 · Strong-Boag 1994 · Granatstein 1998 · McKillop and Palmer 1999 · CHR forum, Buckner, McKay and
Osborne 2000 · Dummitt and Dawson 2009 · coverage through 2000*. Period: period_3.

**Roster:** 19 people: the two prosecutors, seven repliers (four of them women's historians), precursors (Careless, Reid), and the
2000–2009 contributors, with Létourneau as the francophone voice.

**Uptake, measured through the texts.** Any core debate text: 34 of 1,303 strict 2009–2026 PhDs (2.6%); 52 of 1,959 strict
PhDs of all years. Falling: 3.7% → 2.2% → 2.0% (2009–14, 2015–19, 2020–26). Granatstein 1998 is the most cited
(23 strict PhDs on its primary record, 16 since 2009); Bliss 15 (all years; 7 since 2009). The brief's counts match the primary graph
records; merging misspelled variants adds one or two. The brief's "Kealey 1992 (3)" is the Linda Kealey et al. piece, not Gregory
Kealey's (2 PhDs, none since 2009). Of the 34 PhDs citing debate texts, 22 also engage the working-class anchors (lift 2.96) and 19
the women's and gender anchors (lift 2.22).

**Judgement calls.**
- Eight relationships here (a ninth, from `liberal_order_framework`, is proposed in that draft as lof_rel_09). As the brief asked, the debate connects to `social` (critique), `cdn_working_class` and `cdn_women_gender`
  (their replies, critiques toward the debate), and `cdn_regional` (limited identities as background). Also: comparisons with
  `cdn_political_national`, `cdn_national_tradition`, `quebec_historiography` and `truthdebate`.
- The labour replies are title-level evidence only. The women's-history reply is supported by Strong-Boag's 1994 abstract.
- `revival` (in groups.json's related list) is not proposed: no checked text links them.

**Questions for the user:** see `open_questions` (fallback targets, title-level replies, Bliss's placement, the francophone side,
uptake versus teaching value, person-id reconciliation).
