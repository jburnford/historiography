## Judgement calls
- **The debate inside the field.** Laverdure's 1998 review of *A Full-Orbed Christianity* names the two sides: Cook and Marshall's secularization thesis against Christie and Gauvreau. The roster keeps both sides as historians, and the strands separate them.
- **Francophone scholarship.** Hamelin and Gagnon, Ferretti, Hubert, Meunier and Warren, and Bienvenue are included. Gauvreau's review of Bienvenue and Baum's review of Gauvreau carry the Catholic Action argument.
- **Correction.** Lynne Marks's 1996 book is *Revivals and Roller Rinks*, not "Revivals and Roll Call". The Crossref contents confirmed the title.
- **Moral reform** (Valverde) is a comparison here; her primary placement is moral regulation.
- **Unverified and left out:** the French edition of Gauvreau (2008) and Allen's 1961 thesis. (Marshall's 1993–94 essay was verified on 2026-10-12; see below.)
- **Declining engagement:** the share of PhDs citing two or more of the listed works falls from 7.8% (2009–14) to 4.4% (2020–26).

## Questions for the user
- Should the Catholic Action and Quiet Revolution strand live here, in Quebec historiography, or in Canada's 1960s? The draft keeps the works here and links them to both by contribution.
- Are Cook and Marshall well described as the "secularization thesis"? The label comes from Laverdure's 1998 review; Marshall's 1993 historiographical essay (unverified) would give his own account.
- Allen's The Social Passion is cited by only 16 PhDs (2009–26) and is falling; is it still the right founding milestone, or should the label start with Cook (1985)?
- Should Irish Catholic and ethnic religious history (Clarke, McGowan, Perin) move to immigration and ethnicity?
- Engagement falls from 7.8% (2009–14) to 4.4% (2020–26) of PhDs citing two or more of the listed works; should the field be described as declining in the extension coverage note?
