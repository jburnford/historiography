## 1. Sources read

### 1a. Holberg Prize, laureate page "Bruno Latour" (Holberg Prize 2013)
URL https://holbergprize.org/laureates/holbergprize/3661/. **Read in full (committee citation and biography).**
- Citation: "Bruno Latour has undertaken an ambitious analysis and reinterpretation of modernity, challenging the most fundamental categories such as the distinction between modern and pre-modern, nature and society, human and non-human. He has completely re-imagined Science Studies, pioneering new ethnographic methods ... His influence has been felt internationally and well beyond the social study of science, in history, art history, philosophy, anthropology, geography, theology, literature and legal studies."
- On the 1980s: Laboratory Life (1979) "set the standard for ethnographic analyses of the making of scientific facts"; *Les Microbes* (1984) and *Science in Action* (1987) "represents some of the most important work in the field of science studies and in the social history of knowledge"; "In We Have Never Been Modern (1991) Latour offered his major polemical statement"; "His work in the 1980s established Actor Network Theory (ANT) as a widely used methodology".
- Later: Politics of Nature (1999), "in which he proposed 'a parliament of things'" (the committee's attribution); *Inquiry into Modes of Existence* (2012) extends the debate into "creative metaphysics".
- The page lists the 2013 Holberg Symposium "From Economics to Ecology" (4 June 2013) in his honour, and a memoriam: he died on 9 October 2022.

### 1b. Kyoto Prize, laureate page "Bruno Latour" (2021, Arts and Philosophy)
URL https://www.kyotoprize.org/en/en/laureates/bruno_latour/. **Read (profile and the opening of the citation).**
- "revolutionized the conventional view of science by treating nature, humans, laboratory equipment, and other entities as equal actors, and describing technoscience as the hybrid network of these actors. His philosophy re-examines 'modernity' based on the dualism of nature and society. He has a large influence across disciplines, with his multifaceted activities that include proposals regarding global environmental issues."
- With Woolgar he "opened up a new field called 'anthropology of science'"; ANT developed "in collaboration with Michel Callon, John Law, and other researchers" at the Centre de sociologie de l'innovation.
- "Recently, Latour has advocated the necessity of a serious understanding of the Anthropocene"; the "terrestrial".

### 1c. Andrew Pickering, review of *We Have Never Been Modern*, *Modernism/modernity* 1:3 (September 1994), pp. 257–258. DOI 10.1353/mod.1994.0044
**Read in full.**
- "Bruno Latour’s Science in Action (1987) was the most important book on science, technology and their intertwining with society published in the 1980s"; its actor-network analysis "flew in the face of technological and social determinist perspectives" (p. 257).
- The book's argument as Pickering states it: the "modern constitution" bequeathed by Boyle and Hobbes, "the program for purifying the discourses of nature and society"; "'We have never been modern,' as Latour puts it, but we found a way of thinking that we were" (p. 257).
- Criticism: "Latour is explaining too much with his conception of modernity"; "There is a note of complacency here at the end of Latour’s book"; "I would not want to ratify what we have always done without first getting clear on the specifics of history—the history of the factory and labor, say, of gender relations, of militarism, colonialism, empire, and so on. There is no place in Latour’s story for such specifics" (p. 258).

### 1d. Latour, "Gabriel Tarde and the End of the Social", in Patrick Joyce, ed., *The Social in Question: New Bearings in History and the Social Sciences* (London: Routledge, 2002), pp. 117–132
Author's typescript (21 pp.), author's page https://www.bruno-latour.fr/node/181.html (dated 2001 there; a German translation appeared in *Soziale Welt* in 2001). **Read in part: typescript pp. 1–2, 5, 7, 10.**
- ANT is "a deliberate attempt at terminating the use of the word 'social' in social theory to replace it with the word 'association'"; "ANT actually has a forefather, namely Gabriel Tarde" (p. 1).
- "Since then, main stream social theory has never tired of ridiculing Tarde’s achievement and I must confess that I myself never enquired further than the dismissive footnotes of the Durkheimians" (p. 2).
- "Instead of saying, like Durkheim, that we 'should treat social facts as a thing', Tarde says that 'all things are society', and any phenomenon is a social fact" (p. 5).
- "Tarde can be said to have invented microhistory many decades before its discoverers" (p. 7).
- "It is not only the case that Durkheim has taken society as the cause instead of seeing that it is never more than a highly provisional consequence" (p. 10); the Durkheimians "have simply taken the explanandum for the explanans" (p. 10).

### 1f. *Politics of Nature: How to Bring the Sciences into Democracy* (Harvard University Press, 2004; *Politiques de la nature*, La Découverte, 1999)
- **Author's description** (https://www.bruno-latour.fr/node/88.html): "A political philosophy enquiry into the limits of political ecology"; "the various green movements are not modifying the modernist project but giving it, on the contrary, a new lease on life"; new institutions so that "scientific facts" are "elaborated through due process".
- **Antony J. Puddephatt, review, Canadian Journal of Sociology Online (Canadian Sociological Association), September 2004 — read in full (2 pp.).** URL https://www.csa-scs.ca/files/www/crs/documents/reviews/archives/pdf/200409LATOUR.pdf. Quotes Latour (p. 5): "at no time in its short history has political ecology ever had anything to do with nature, with its defense or its protection". Judgement: "Despite the cryptic style of delivery, an almost endless use of new jargon, and an overall lack of empirical rigor, the book nevertheless contains highly insightful, original, and compelling propositions"; Latour "relies too much on pure armchair theorizing".
- The book is **unread**.

### 1h. *An Inquiry into Modes of Existence: An Anthropology of the Moderns*, trans. Catherine Porter (Harvard University Press, August 2013; *Enquête sur les modes d'existence. Une anthropologie des Modernes*, La Découverte, September 2012)
- **Author's description** (https://www.bruno-latour.fr/node/252.html): "it offers a positive version to the question raised, only negatively, with the publication, in 1991, of ''We have never been modern'': if ''we'' have never been modern, then what have ''we'' been?"; "a research protocol ... very different from the actor-network theory"; "a new philosophical anthropology"; an ERC grant (2011–2014) and a digital platform.
- **Chapter 1, "Defining the Object of Inquiry", pp. 27–46, read in full** (the publisher's pages as posted by the author): "LET US IMAGINE AN ANTHROPOLOGIST WHO HAS COME UP WITH THE IDEA OF RECONSTITUTING THE VALUE SYSTEM OF 'WESTERN societies'" (p. 27); she must "focus on the very heart of modern institutions—science, the economy, politics, law, and so on—rather than on the margins" (p. 27); she resists "OCCIDENTALISM, a form of EXOTICISM applied to what is close at hand, which consists in believing what the West says about itself, whether in praise or criticism" (p. 27); "only a prolonged, in-depth analysis of COURSES OF ACTION can allow her to discover the real value system" (pp. 27–28); "a contemporary anthropologist has to learn to talk about her subjects of study to her subjects of study" (p. 45); "an anthropologist who is not afraid of running the risks of diplomacy" (p. 45).
- No review of the book was read. Reception is therefore the Holberg committee's sentence only.

### 1i. "Agency at the Time of the Anthropocene", *New Literary History* 45:1 (2014), 1–18. DOI 10.1353/nlh.2014.0003
Author's typescript (18 pp.), author's page https://www.bruno-latour.fr/node/501.html. **Read in full.** Prepared for the Holberg Prize Symposium 2013, "From Economics to Ecology", Bergen, 4 June 2013 (typescript p. 18 note).
- "the Earth has now taken back all the characteristics of a full-fledged actor. Indeed, as Dipesh Chakrabarty has proposed, it has become once again an agent of history, or rather, an agent of what I have proposed to call our common geostory" (typescript p. 3; n. 6 cites "The Climate of History: Four Theses").
- "While the older problem of science studies was to understand the active role of scientists in the construction of facts, a new problem arises: how to understand the active role of human agency not only in the construction of facts, but also in the very existence of the phenomena those facts are trying to document?" (p. 2).
- "through a surprising inversion of background and foreground, it is human history that has become frozen and natural history that is taking on a frenetic pace" (p. 13).
- "The prefix 'geo' in geostory does not stand for the return to nature, but for the return of object and subject back to the ground" (p. 17).

### 1j. Latour and Dipesh Chakrabarty, "Conflicts of Planetary Proportion – A Conversation", *Journal of the Philosophy of History* 14:3 (2020), 419–454. DOI 10.1163/18722636-12341450 (online 19 November 2020)
Authors' pre-publication typescript (titled "Conflicts of planetary proportions"), author's site. **Read in part: opening remarks and Latour's pp. 1–4; Chakrabarty's response pp. 19–22.**
- Joint opening: Latour responds to Chakrabarty's "The Planet: An Emergent Humanist Category" (Critical Inquiry, 2019); "our desire to do so arose from the history of an actual and ongoing conversation in which we have been engaged for a while" (p. 1).
- Latour: questions "I’d like to raise in this note before the question is tackled by Dipesh Chakrabarty, a genuine historian"; "Chakrabarty’s introduction of the Planetary triggered a seism in philosophy of history" (p. 3); "Geohistory breaks down any claim to have a human-oriented history" (p. 4); the aim is "to give a spatial and geopolitical ground to counteract the notion of the arrow of time implied so far by traditional philosophies of history" (p. 4).
- Chakrabarty: "Much of the ground my own thoughts move on is shared with Latour"; he will "suggest some friendly amendments to what Latour proposes" (p. 21); both indebted to Christophe Bonneuil for "regimes of planetarity" (n. 50).

### 1k. *Facing Gaia: Eight Lectures on the New Climatic Regime* (Polity, July 2017; *Face à Gaïa*, La Découverte, 6 October 2015) and *Down to Earth: Politics in the New Climatic Regime* (Polity, 2018; *Où atterrir ?*, La Découverte, October 2017)

Down to Earth: "define politics as what leads toward the Earth and not toward the global or the national". Both books **unread**.
