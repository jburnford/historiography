## P12. For p4B_rel_16 (settler_colonialism ↔ environment): evidence to merge into Agent B's note

- Cronon 1995 (author-hosted reflow, p. 10, read 2026-10-13): "The removal of Indians to create an 'uninhabited wilderness'" shows "just how invented, just how constructed, the American wilderness really is"; "wilderness represents a flight from history".
- Spence 1999 (OUP abstract, read 2026-10-23): "the early conception of the wilderness as the place where Indians lived (or should live)" gave way to "the idealization of uninhabited wilderness"; removal at Yellowstone, Glacier and Yosemite, 1870s–1930s.
- Binnema and Niemi 2006 (abstract, read): the Stoney were excluded from Banff (1890–1920) for game conservation, sport hunting, tourism and assimilation, "and probably" the same in the United States; not for a wilderness ideal.
- Sandlos 2007 (UBC Press description, read): game laws, parks and sanctuaries "helped the state assert authority over traditional hunting cultures"; Jacoby's blurb.
- Piper and Sandlos 2007 (abstract, read): ecological imperialism limited in the North — "the Canadian North was never subject to the broad ecological transformation that occurred further to the south".
- Daschuk 2013 (press page, read): "Old World diseases, climate, and, most disturbingly, Canadian politics"; blurbed by J. R. McNeill (*Mosquito Empires*); in Canadian PhD bibliographies it enters the environmental founders' neighbourhood in 2015–19 (support 6, lift 4.1) and the settler-colonial neighbourhood in 2020–25.
