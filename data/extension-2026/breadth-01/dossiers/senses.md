# Sensory history: first breadth dossier

**Partial evidence review; staging only. Two selected works do not establish field coverage through 2026.**

A sensory approach crosses fields; a 2021 manifesto is an intervention, not a field-origin date.

Smith’s introduction argues for sensory history as a wider historical practice. The taste/science abstract offers a specific knowledge-making problem. This pairing supports reading senses as historically organized practices rather than appending sensory details to existing narratives.

## Selected evidence

- [A Sensory History Manifesto](https://www.psupress.org/sample_chapter/Smith_SensoryHistoryIntroduction.pdf) (2021): **passage_checked**. Smith argues that sensory history should become a general historical habit while retaining critical debate about historically formed sensory experience. Locator: Introduction, printed pp. 1–5.
- [Taste and the History of Science: Introduction](https://doi.org/10.1017/bjt.2022.8) (2022): **abstract_checked**. Hendriksen and Wragge-Morley challenge the exclusion of taste from histories of scientific knowledge and connect sensory practice with knowledge production. Locator: Abstract, extracted lines 471–476.
