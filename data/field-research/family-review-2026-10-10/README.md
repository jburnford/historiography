# Critical review of the historiography families

The family explorer now exposes substantial teaching content across the project.
The remaining problems concern uneven methodological stories, hidden qualifications
and navigation through large families. Adding more famous names alone will not
resolve them. The highest research priorities are oral-history collaboration,
computer-assisted history before 2000, diplomatic and international history, and
the independent foundations of traditions currently grouped under empire and global
history. These are editorial recommendations, not accepted new historical claims.

This review covers the ten other families, with Indigenous histories as a comparator.
Its baseline is graph revision 1.127: 137 entries, 777 relationships, 940 sources,
909 shared people and 792 approaches. All 77 group entries and their approaches
are inventoried. Existing geographic scope remains primarily Western academic
historiography with selected independent and critical traditions. Regional gaps
below identify consequential limits within that selection, rather than demand
exhaustive world coverage.

## Confirmed problems in the family view

**Source scope is incompletely displayed.** `sourceList()` renders legacy notes and
verification notes, but omits `scope_note` and the newer claim-source witness.
Consequently a source panel can report unrecorded verification even when the
catalogue preserves a checked, narrowly scoped witness. Forty-six distinct sources
referenced by group-level source lists have scope notes. Examples include the
selected introduction to Cohen and Rosenzweig, Harris's selected pages in
*Making Native Space*, and selected passages in Cohen's queer-politics article.
Exact claim details can retain these limits elsewhere; this is a source-panel
problem, not a conclusion that those claims lack evidence. Display the recorded
scope and distinguish descriptions, metadata and primary passages without upgrading
their evidentiary status.

**Field context is hidden.** Family sections show the label and approaches but omit
the entry type, date label and scope note until the full entry is opened. This hides
qualifications about European military coverage, partial digital-history milestones
and public-history institutional gaps. A compact context block should expose these
existing qualifications. Calling every member a field also obscures the difference
between a research field and an intellectual resource.

**Long pages obstruct comparison.** All eleven families render expected records at
desktop and phone widths, with no horizontal overflow or browser errors in 22
contexts. However, the cultural family is approximately 125,000 pixels tall on the
tested phone viewport, with people and connections far below its approach sections.
Add a field index, jump links and an in-family field filter or optional collapsed
sections. Successful rendering does not establish student usability.

**Relationships inside a family are hard to discover.** The explorer exposes
boundary relationships but internal group relationships remain in the timeline or
entry pages. Social history has 47 and cultural history 71 internal links. Make
these existing, qualified links discoverable near their member sections; retain
critique and comparison semantics, exact approach targets and direction. Adjacent
cards must not imply agreement or intellectual descent.

## Historical findings by family

### Social history

Earlier foundations are already present: the Webbs, Alice Clark, Ivy Pinchbeck,
Black historical collecting and institutional work, labour history and regional
microhistory. This is not a field that simply begins in the 1960s. The large total
nevertheless masks very small racial-formation and intersectionality presentations.
Review Black feminist foundations and their specific historical applications.
Patricia Hill Collins already occurs as a local candidate referent in the Smith
claim catalogue; she is not absent from every layer. Combahee is mentioned in
existing source context, but mention is not an original-statement method card.
Reuse these records and verify editions and passages before expanding them.
Collins's institutional biography distinguishes the 1990 book and its 2000 revision.
[University of Maryland](https://socy.umd.edu/facultyprofile/collins/patricia-hill)

Oral history is the most immediately actionable gap. Its roster contains Julie
Cruikshank, Angela Sidney, Kitty Smith, Annie Ned, Angela Cavender Wilson, Silvia
Rivera Cusicanqui, Elizabeth Lapovsky Kennedy, Madeline Davis, Alice Lynd, Staughton
Lynd and Betty Balanoff without direct approach assignment in that entry. Several
have already scoped method sources. Add bounded collaborative approaches where
those passages support them, retaining narrator, knowledge contributor, researcher
and editor roles. Searching Cruikshank currently finds her profile but no approach.
The Indigenous comparator demonstrates a useful representation pattern.

### Cultural and intellectual history

Earlier cultural foundations, feminist history, contextualism, conceptual history,
language, book culture and historical anthropology are substantial. The family
also mixes broad philosophical resources with research practices, making a large
roster a poor measure of disciplinary balance. Distinguish those roles in the view
and show historical applications rather than multiplying theoretical ancestors.
Religious, art, emotions and book-history publication topics need an existing-content
crosswalk before new field proposals.

Independent intellectual historiographies deserve a bounded review. Romila Thapar's
own university CV describes early Indian social and cultural inquiry and lists
*From Lineage to State* and *Cultural Pasts*. This establishes a research lead, not
an influence relationship to Cambridge or a checked reading of those books.
[JNU curriculum vitae](https://jnu.ac.in/sites/default/files/RomilaThapar.pdf)

### Annales

The current content includes the interwar founders and journal, earlier historical
problems, serial economic work, Braudel, the later critical turn and methodological
variation. The earlier missing-origin issue has been repaired. Hilton and Genet's
uncarded contextual or critical roles do not by themselves constitute omissions.
Preserve Annales as its own family, as previously approved. Outstanding early
publication-backfile gaps are corpus acquisition problems, not evidence that the
school lacked interwar work. This family is lower priority for another expansion.

### Political national and international history

Political inquiry is represented through institutional, popular, cultural and
rights-related approaches, with earlier and non-European examples. International
history receives much less distinct treatment: diplomatic and international history
is a record-only topic and its teaching representation is largely a single
diplomacy/conflict approach. Investigate a bounded sequence of archival diplomacy,
First World War responsibility debates, Cold War revisionism and later cultural or
non-state approaches. Finney's institutional historiographical essay supplies an
initial framework; individual works and exchanges still need passage checks.
[Institute of Historical Research](https://archives.history.ac.uk/makinghistory/resources/articles/international_history.html)

### War and the military

Battle, strategy, naval history, recruitment, gender, labour and memory are already
present. European and Anglophone emphasis is explicitly qualified in the data but
less visible in the family view. Nine of 25 approaches belong to the approved
Holocaust bridge; retain that bridge while making clear that it does not describe
the entire military field. A bounded Pacific-war and colonial-war review is more
useful than adding another generic strategist. John Dower's 1986 and 1999 books
are confirmed bibliographic leads, with method and reception awaiting reading.
[MIT faculty biography](https://history.mit.edu/people/john-w-dower/)

### Economy and social science history

Coverage already includes quantitative disputes, institutions, labour, consumption,
dependency and Brazilian, Indian and Chinese economic history. Business history
is record-only in the publication grouping, yet Chandler's work is already in the
economic teaching material. Link it before declaring an absent tradition. Explain
the relation between economic fields, social-science methods and theoretical
resources; their current shared family does not establish a single lineage.
Prioritize existing counterfactual, slavery and model debates for comparison.
Agricultural publication-backfile gaps remain separate from teaching-content gaps.

### Empire colonialism and the global

African, Caribbean and South Asian historical foundations are not limited to
postwar academic schools: earlier work and oral historical knowledge are already
represented. The family label nevertheless risks making independent African and
anti-colonial historiographies look like branches of settler imperial scholarship.
Expose their distinct questions and institutions within the approved grouping.
Review J. F. Ade Ajayi's institutional place in the Ibadan School as a candidate;
Ibadan's own memorial supports that institutional context, without settling the
method of an unread book. [University of Ibadan memorial](https://bulletins.ui.edu.ng/sites/default/files/2025-03/ui_bulletin_09sept2014_0_0.pdf)

Several postcolonial roster participants have relevant work in other entries.
Audit Cohn, Prakash, Hall and Gilroy for precise cross-links before producing new
generic influence arrows. Imperial, migration, transnational, slavery and maritime
record-only subjects require the same crosswalk discipline.

### Science technology and medicine

The data already reaches beyond a Kuhn-centred story: Chinese scholarship and
collaborator credits, Arabic, Indian and African inquiries, feminist history and
older social medicine are represented. Fleck's roster presence without a distinct
method card is a focused research lead; do not convert a title or roster role into
established transmission. Medical coverage should be reviewed for patient,
clinical, psychiatric and institutional methods before 2000. Disability remains a
record-only topic. Selected later works do not complete those earlier gaps.

### Environment

The family includes earlier geographical foundations, multiple Dust Bowl
interpretations, Indian, South African and Mexican work, and Bullard's 1990
environmental-justice intervention. Neither a solely 1970s origin nor an absent
justice tradition is a defensible criticism. More comparative regional practice
would be useful alongside the detailed Dust Bowl sequence. Warren Dean's Brazilian
forest history is a candidate: distinguish the 1995 first edition from the 1997
paperback and read its historical method before acceptance.
[UC Press paperback and description](https://www.ucpress.edu/book/9780520208865/with-broadax-and-firebrand)

### Theory and method

The family contains important older archives and philosophical resources but
uneven accounts of professional practice. Digital and web approaches all have
selected intervention dates after 2000. Those dates are explicitly milestones,
not origin claims, yet the absence of earlier practice is consequential for a
twentieth-century historiography project. Review computing, record linkage,
prosopography and electronic publishing before 2000, connecting the existing
quantitative and spatial approaches where evidence supports it. A participant's
account documents the 1986 history-and-computing conference; it should not settle
a separate constitutional founding date. RRCHNM's institutional account records
the 1993 *Who Built America?* CD-ROM and early collaborative projects. Preserve
ASHP and other collaborators rather than assigning the whole field to one founder.
[AHA participant account](https://www.historians.org/perspectives-article/history-and-computing-a-learned-society-and-a-european-perspective/),
[RRCHNM institutional history](https://20.rrchnm.org/about.html)

Public history's professional movement needs to remain distinct from much older
museum, archive, historical-society and government practice. Review those earlier
institutions and community collaborations alongside the existing university
programmes. NCPH makes that distinction explicitly.
[NCPH field account](https://ncph.org/what-is-public-history/about-the-field/)
Oral-history changes above also serve this family through its bridge entry.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Expose existing source scope and field context; add usable navigation and access
   to internal relationships. Preserve the exact-2000 snapshot and every source's
   evidence limit. These are display corrections, with no historical promotion.
2. Build a small oral-history collaboration batch from already scoped sources,
   then verify computing and electronic publishing before 2000. Each proposed
   approach needs distinct contributor roles, dated witnesses and passage locators.
3. Review international history, Black feminist historical methods and independent
   African and Indian historiographical programmes. Candidate metadata starts
   research; it does not authorize accepted method or influence claims.
4. Make the record-only crosswalk, then select regional military, medical and
   environmental additions according to demonstrable methodological differences.
   Keep venue acquisition work in its separate coverage queue.
5. Use student testing to assess discoverability and comparison, including finding
   a community collaborator, recognizing a source's unread body, identifying a
   bridge and navigating a large family on a phone.
