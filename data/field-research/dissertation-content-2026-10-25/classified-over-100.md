## Threshold tradeoff and evidence

Raising the Canadian-history screen from >100 to ≥150 leaves 7 of 36 historian-class rows on that line; 29 fall below 150. This moderates the corpus’s Canada-heavy sampling, but does not establish that Carter 152 is more important than Christie 149, nor that excluded Canadian work is adequately developed. Namesake and mixed cases are held separately.

Alan Taylor’s northern-borderland studies and Michael B. Katz’s Hamilton study cross the focus boundary. They remain in the other-history line because of their broader early-American/US social-history contexts; Angus McLaren is on the Canadian-history line because the current curated context specifically uses his Canadian eugenics and reproductive-policy works. These are explicit editorial choices, open to work-level refinement.

Current curated context and frozen bibliographic titles ground the classifications; body texts were not newly read for every name. For uncertain roles and identities, the review consulted primary university, author and publisher bibliographies.
