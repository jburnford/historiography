Like for like, 2010–: Canada and the United States weighted by programme

The United States corpus over-represents universities with open repositories. Here both countries are limited to PhDs from 2010 on, and each United States PhD is weighted so that institutions count in proportion to their completed History PhDs in the AHA directory, decade by decade (scripts/dissertation_weights.py). United States: 2,088 PhDs at 36 institutions, effective sample about 1,234. Canada: 1,347 PhDs, unweighted. Weighting cannot add programmes we hold nothing from: since 2010 the institutions held award 35% of US History PhDs, and these top-20 programmes have none: Univ. of Chicago (326); Princeton Univ. (324); Columbia Univ. (320); Univ. of Texas, Austin (247); Ohio State Univ., Columbus (196); Georgetown Univ. (190); Rutgers, The State Univ. of NJ (184); Graduate Center, CUNY (183); Univ. of Illinois, Urbana-Champaign (178); Northwestern Univ. (174).

Notes and limits

  • Only strict History PhDs count; non-History and unparsed theses are excluded.
  • Works are resolved citation records; a few title variants remain unmerged, so counts are slight undercounts.
  • The United States corpus leans towards universities with open repositories and towards the 2010s; several of the largest programmes are missing (see the weighted section). Families cover only theses that cite at least 3 widely cited works, which under-represents older theses.
  • Source data: works.csv, cited-people.csv, dissertations.csv and summary/countries/<CC>/tfidf-families/.
