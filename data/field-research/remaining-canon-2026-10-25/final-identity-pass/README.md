# Final identity pass: Brown, Taylor and Smith

Checked 2026-10-25. This is an additive review of three holds in the frozen 21-case identity/classification review. It does not change the release, the earlier review, a graph identity, or a public citation total.

| Intended person | Frozen dated key | Confirmed lower bound | Optimistic upper bound | Outcome |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Charles Taylor, philosopher born 1931 | 148 | 102 | 141 | Qualifies for the theory queue |
| Peter Robert Lamont Brown, late-antique historian born 1935 | 114 | 101 | 112 | Qualifies for the other-history queue |
| Adam Smith, eighteenth-century moral philosopher | 110 | 77 | 100 | Cannot meet the strict >100 screen |

Each number is a UNION of dated dissertation IDs within the exact frozen first-author work keys. Work counts are never added. The lower bound includes only positively attributed work rows; the upper includes every remaining held row and excludes only confirmed foreign works. The 6,794-dissertation population uses `in_analysis=True` and a nonempty year. Neither all-authorship totals nor an inference about complete reading is substituted.

`review.json` gives an explicit decision for all 174 work rows (Taylor 75, Brown 61, Smith 38), retaining every raw field. `dated-citation-sets.json` records each work's dated ID set and each intended person's lower, upper and frozen sets. `sources.json` states the extent of each metadata check; earlier source records and their qualifications are copied rather than rewritten. `count-witnesses.json` and `validation.json` report the exact recomputation. `additive-queue.json` requests the two qualifying profiles separately from any future biography implementation.

Taylor's conservative 102 uses 17 clear rows. PUL and BnF identify *Rapprocher les solitudes* as the McGill philosopher's collected essays, presented by Guy Laforest; its publisher/catalogue manifestation dates differ and remain qualified. BAnQ identifies Charlotte Melançon's *Sources du moi* translation and its 1998 original manifestation. McGill's bibliography, Princeton's volume and essay credits, and Duke's chapter contents establish the other selected rows. Amy Gutmann's editorship and the other commentators' chapters remain distinct from Taylor's central essay. The early exploratory estimate of 107 is not the final count: date-conflicting and OCR-title variants remain held here. *Radical Tories* is the journalist namesake's work and remains excluded.

Brown's 101 uses 31 rows. The additional positives are specific French, Portuguese and Spanish books, the German *Spätantike* chapter, two early articles and individually credited chapters. Original works, translations, collections and later manifestations retain their separate dates and credits. In particular, a frozen French *À travers un trou d'aiguille* row carries the English original's 2012 date, whereas its French publisher records the 2016 translation; it is attributed to Brown's work family without repairing that record. The 1972 *O Fim do Mundo Clássico* row is matched at title/author-family level by a current publisher explicitly identifying the Princeton historian; that later page does not verify the earlier manifestation date. The Hume Brown Scottish-travel row remains foreign. Other unchecked Brown namesakes and fragments remain explicitly unassigned rather than being silently attributed or excluded.

Smith's upper bound falls from 104 to exactly 100 after four further foreign rows: modern US historian Adam I. P. Smith's *Emergence of Conservatism* and the *Contested Legacy* chapter coauthored with Peter J. Parish; Adam D. Smith's 2017 Chinese-manuscript article; and Jonathan M. Smith's 2018 *Shun* article. The last is neither the eighteenth-century philosopher nor Adam D. Smith. Every untranslated/unchecked title and fragment remains held; even assigning them all to the intended philosopher cannot produce >100, so no speculative translation matches are needed to decide eligibility.

The threshold reflects historical/theoretical function and project focus. Taylor's Canadian birthplace does not place his theoretical-resource profile on the Canadian-history ≥150 line. No QID is guessed, no common-name alias is merged, and source-key totals remain historical facts about those keys.

It reads the release, checks its manifest, verifies that all 1,072 original work rows equal the frozen release, independently rebuilds the 174 work ID sets, compares them with the earlier sets, and confirms the old artifact hashes are unchanged.
