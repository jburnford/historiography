# JSTOR full text: coverage and note-parser pilot (2026-11-06)

Counts only. No OCR, note or review text is quoted here; samples and audit sheets stay in the session scratchpad.

## 1. Coverage (deliverable 1)

`jstor_ft_coverage.py stream` made one pass over the delivery (8 min). It wrote `signals.tsv` and two shards to `/mnt/e/jstor-fulltext-2026-11-06/`:
- `reviews.jsonl.gz`: 317,560 book reviews, 0.9 GB;
- `noref_items.jsonl.gz`: items with no JSTOR note list, 0.4 GB.

`jstor_ft_coverage.py aggregate` writes `coverage.tsv`, one row per journal × decade.

| | items | JSTOR note list | notes only in the text | no notes found |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| research articles | 108,343 | 90,877 (84%) | 3,342 | 14,124 |
| review essays and review articles | 1,730 | 189 | 58 | 1,483 |
| misc, news and other | 23,674 | 114 | 3,585 | 19,975 |

- **How "notes only in the text" is detected:** imprints, plus Ibid./op. cit., plus a third of the page references, at five or more per ten pages (rule B). The rule was calibrated on articles that have ≥ 10 JSTOR notes, since their text carries the same notes. Recall is 70% before 1950, 84% for 1950–99 and 81% from 2000. The true number of noted articles without a list is therefore about 4,000–4,500.
- **Hand check of "no notes found":** 25 sampled articles were almost all non-research items, such as bibliographies, comments and replies, genealogies, edited documents, exhibit reviews and archive guides. Misses are mostly German-style footnotes (`Ort Jahr, S.`), which this rule does not see well.
- **Hand check of "notes only in the text":** 12 of 12 sampled items have real notes. Many are born-digital articles from the 2000s and 2010s, stored as one "page" with all their notes at the end, which JSTOR did not split.
- **Bibliography lists:** 2,805 items without a note list have bibliography-type titles (bibliographies, books received, theses lists).
- **Where text parsing matters most:** the 2000s and 2010s (862 and 856 articles), *History of Education Quarterly* (401), *Isis* (350), *Pacific Historical Review* (305), *Journal of Modern History* (273) and *Law and History Review* (222).
- **Misc items with notes in the text:** these include *Revue Historique* (942), *Isis* (253) and *HAHR* (237). They may be research articles deposited under another genre.

## 2. Note parser pilot (deliverable 2, rules stage)

`jstor_notes.py` turns one item's `references` list into citations, in note order. It builds on the dissertation parsers (`scripts/extract_dissertation_notes.py` and `scripts/extract_dissertation_bibliographies.py`).

- **Rules:** JATS XML; Ibid.; idem and dash authors; op. cit. and cross-note references `(n. 3)`; short forms; author-date (resolved against the item's own bibliography entries); inverted, period-style and MLA bibliography entries; `(Place: Publisher, Year)` books; quoted articles and chapters; French, Italian and Spanish comma imprints; German `Ort Jahr`; periodical issues; archival and primary sources (legal cases, interviews, archive call numbers); fragments without an author.
- **Each row carries:** rule id, kind, confidence, and `ref` for back-references.

**Sample:** one article per journal per 20-year span (325 articles, 30,103 citations). The run took 3.5 s, so all 8.0M pieces would take about 20–30 min on CPU.

| outcome | share | before 1940 | 1940–79 | 1980 on |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| full citation, confidence ≥ 0.7 | 28% | 5% | 20% | 33% |
| full citation, lower confidence | 7% | 2% | 5% | 7% |
| partial (no author, title or year) | 8% | 11% | 9% | 7% |
| back-reference resolved (Ibid., short form, author-date) | 16% | 7% | 17% | 17% |
| back-reference unresolved | 13% | 28% | 16% | 11% |
| primary or archival | 7% | 9% | 6% | 7% |
| other or unparsed | 20% | 37% | 26% | 17% |

**Hand audits:** three rounds of 60 random parsed rows, each round on a fresh seed. Fixes followed each round.

| round | all classes correct (authors, title, year, kind) | full citations with confidence ≥ 0.7 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 46/60 | |
| 2 | about 45/60 | |
| 3 | about 44/60 | 19/24 |

Every round surfaced new tail forms rather than repeats. Errors cluster in notes from before 1950 and in continental notes:
- German `Hg.`-first citations;
- possessive commentary ("X's articles, …");
- government reports;
- OCR-damaged guillemets.

**Unresolved back-references:** 2,598 of 3,937 have the surname in an earlier note whose full form did not parse or match; 1,263 have no earlier mention (the first citation was lost, or sits in the article text).

**Conclusion:** the rules handle the clean, modern half well. The older half, the unparsed and partial rows and the unresolved back-references need a second pass, either an LLM on Nibi (as in the topic-model jobs) or more rules per journal and era.
