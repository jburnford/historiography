# Qwen pilots on Nibi: notes and review headings (2026-11-07)

Model: Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 with vLLM on one H100 (`jstor_llm_notes.py`, `jstor_llm_reviews.py`, greedy decoding,
max_num_seqs 64). The answers and the hand-audit sheets stay local because they quote text. This note has counts only.

## Review headings and signatures (job 26351198)

- **Sample:** 464 book reviews, 141 of them without a JSTOR title. 456 answered and 8 failed to parse as JSON.
- **Time:** 436 s including model load; the job took 13 min with startup. That is about 1.1 reviews/s.

| on the 456 answered | rules | Qwen |
|---|---:|---:|
| reviews with at least one book named | 330 | 444 |
| reviews with a `strong` / `title+year` match to one graph work (no thesis, article or review targets) | 94 | 151 |
| … of them untitled reviews | 36 | 46 |
| reviews with a reviewer | 322 | 432 |

- **Books:** 290 of the 447 rule heading books have a close title in the Qwen answer.
  - A hand check of 12 strict disagreements: the rules were wrong in 8. The errors were the previous review or a running head taken into the title, a subtitle taken as the title, a translator taken as the author, and a degree ('Dr. phil.') taken as the title. Qwen was wrong in none.
  - In 3 of the 12, the books were the same and Qwen had corrected the OCR ('Iidians' → 'Indians').
  - 12 of 12 sampled books that only Qwen found were right.
- **Reviewers:** on the 321 reviews where both give a name, they agree on 309. Most of the other 12 are rule short forms ('Dr. Risse') for the same person.
  - Qwen names 111 reviewers that the rules miss, and most of the ones checked are right.
  - With `where` null, Qwen sometimes seems to take a name from the creators list without having read it. Keep a Qwen reviewer only when `where` is set.

## Note citations (job 26350911)

- **Sample:** 60 articles stratified by era, 581 requests of up to 8 note pieces each (4,443 pieces). 579 answered and 2 failed.
- **Time:** 1,200 s including load, about 3.7 pieces/s. Output averaged about 115 tokens per piece, and output is the cost.

`jstor_llm_eval.py` outcome classes, as a share of citations:

| era | | full | backref resolved | backref unresolved | partial | primary | other |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all | rules | 29% | 15% | 12% | 12% | 7% | 24% |
| all | Qwen | 33% | 19% | 11% | 8% | 29% | – |
| pre-1940 | rules | 6% | 8% | 25% | 14% | 10% | 37% |
| pre-1940 | Qwen | 12% | 13% | 20% | 13% | 42% | – |
| 1980+ | rules | 47% | 15% | 6% | 11% | 4% | 17% |
| 1980+ | Qwen | 51% | 18% | 7% | 5% | 20% | – |

- **Graph edges.** These count distinct (article, graph work) pairs from `strong` / `title+year` matches to exactly one work:

  | | edges |
  |---|---:|
  | rules | 230 |
  | Qwen | 300 |
  | both | 227 |
  | rules only | 3 |
  | Qwen only | 73 (+32%) |

  - 20 of 20 sampled Qwen-only edges are plausible for their citing journals.
  - The gains are mostly from pieces the rules leave unparsed or partial: theses, 'Author, Title, Place Year' forms, citations inside prose, and pre-1940 notes.
- **Hand audit of 40 random pieces:**
  - Qwen was right on about 37.
  - Its misses: one piece skipped, and one year taken from context rather than from the note (the archive citation date replaced by the event year).
  - It sometimes fills in a given name not printed in the note ('Maitland, F. W.').
  - The rules were right on about 28. Their misses: theses read as articles or books, editors and titles in German and French notes, and citations inside prose.

## Cost of a full run

- **Reviews:** 317,560 come to about 55–80 GPU-hours, depending on how much of the 436 s was model load (one load per array task). The reviews the rules did not match come to about 40–55.
- **Notes:** 8.33M pieces at 3.7/s comes to about 625 GPU-hours.
  - Sending only the articles where the rules leave many pieces unparsed, partial or unresolved, mostly before 1980, roughly halves that.
  - A compact answer format (no null fields) would cut output tokens further.
