People

Who the people are

Person keys are not all historians. By the evidence found for each key: historians, scholars from other fields, people who appear as primary sources, and keys with too few facts to tell. Duplicate keys still count separately here.

Evidence found for each person key

Keys showing each signal; one key can show several. Only the key log's own claims count here: credits linked by rules are never used as evidence about who a person is. A historian with none of the historian signals on record is not yet counted as one.

How well each person is attested

Score: 2 per public identifier family (LC, Wikidata, ORCID), 1 per historian signal, up to 3 for the theses and articles (JSTOR notes, Crossref reference lists) citing their works (5, 20, 100), up to 2 for works (3, 10), up to 3 for network partners (1, 5, 20). Strong 6+, supported 3–5, thin 2 or less. Also thin: fewer than 5 citing theses or articles, no public identifier and no partners. Many thin keys duplicate a stronger key.

Works

Works by kind

Every work in the graph, in two layers. Works with a project identifier have a key in the project's key log; works held as references only are records from LC, OpenAlex or Crossref that the graph links to (cited works, works on scholars' profiles) without minting a key. Each work counts under one kind.

Where the books come from

Book works by the record that holds them, each counted once: the LC catalogue first, then Crossref or OpenAlex, then the edited volumes that chapter records name.

With a project identifierHeld as references only

Where the reference-only works come from

How the works without a project identifier entered the graph. They carry citations, credits and places like any work, but no key of their own; a work gets a key once the key log holds it.

Who wrote what: author credits

A credit ties a person key to a work. Some are the key log's own claims, reviewed one by one or by audited rules; others are linked from outside records by rules or identifiers. Linked credits are shown in the graph but never used as evidence in identity decisions.

Historians born
