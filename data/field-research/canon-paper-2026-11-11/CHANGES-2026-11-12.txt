Canon paper: what changed when the tables were rerun on the build of 2026-11-12 (morning)

The tables of 2026-11-11 evening (made on the build of 11-11 20:08) are kept in out-2026-11-11-evening/ (a copy of out/
before this run). The tables in out/ are now from the live build/graph written 2026-11-12 about 05:00 (42.14% of thesis
citations matched). Counts and shares only; distinct citing documents throughout; person keys are never summed.
"People" below means person keys: they include other-field scholars and authors of primary sources, not only historians.

1. What changed

In the graph since the evening run:
- Roles on AUTHORED and EDITED credits (data/graph/edition-roles.csv.gz). Edition editors, translators, introducers,
  series editors and (from 2026-11-12) contributors, that is qualified editors such as associate, assistant, consulting
  or picture editors, do not count as citations of the person's own work. In 00_base.py they are how = 'edition_role',
  left out of hows(): 22,670 credits.
- Volume editors from LC and Crossref (linked EDITED rows); first publication years on works (first_year, 62,548 works
  whose first year differs from the key year); 1,749 canonical keys that were translations now roll up to the original
  (edition-canonical-fixes); 227 LC-author-conflict linked credits demoted (Di Tella); co-authorship only from
  author-role credits; the journal-metadata matches (5,572 thesis citations).
In the scripts:
- 00_base.py: the match flag adds the journal-metadata crosswalk last, with its guards, as export_cited_works.py does:
  1,285,599 of 3,050,449 matched (42.14%, the build's figure; was 1,280,027, 41.96%). The roll-up key (gkey) follows
  data/graph/edition-canonical-fixes.csv.gz as build_graph does (2,063 keys with one proposal; 3,114 corpus works now
  point to a different original); gkey_cited is unchanged. gw.year is now coalesce(first_year, year); gw.key_year keeps
  the key's own year. 'contributor' added to the edition roles.
- 05_age.py: docstring only; every graph-year measure now reads gw.year, i.e. the first publication year.
- 08_data_checks.py: anachronistic pairs counted by the first year and, as before, by the key year.
Reconstruction check: the corpus-to-graph map gives 1,227,716 (thesis, work) pairs, 1,226,940 of them also graph CITES
edges; the graph holds 1,240,515 thesis pairs, so 13,575 graph edges (older cites.csv.gz routes) are not rebuilt from
the crosswalks (evening: 13,120). 10,068 matched citations (579 works) still have no live graph key.

2. The five claims (FINDINGS.md numbering)

Claim 1, the canon reaches far but takes little. STANDS; figures move by tenths of a point.
- Top-100 works' share of thesis citations: corpus 0.66% before and after; graph 2.04% (1.99-2.09) -> 2.06% (2.01-2.11).
- Theses citing a top-100 work: corpus 46.4% (unchanged), graph 56.4% -> 56.8%.
- Theses citing one of the 100 most-cited people: graph 72.4% -> 72.3%; corpus 73.0% (unchanged).
- Top-100 people's share of person pairs (graph): 5.80% -> 5.86%. Person pairs fall from 797,683 to 777,738 because
  edition-role credits no longer count.

Claim 2, supervisor effect. STANDS, unchanged.
- Own supervisor cited through matched works: 48.0% (46.7-49.4) before and after; with parsed names 56.7% both.
  Cited outside own students 47.9% -> 48.0%. Same 8,950 pairs from 7,741 theses.
- Topical peers (same institution and decade, 3+ shared works): 10.9% -> 10.8%; ratio 4.4 (4.2-4.6) both.
- All institution-decade peers: 2.01% -> 2.00%; ratio 23.9 -> 24.0.

Claim 3, Anglophone canons overlap about a fifth. STANDS.
- Top-100 people shared (route A): CA-US 20 -> 21, GB-US 26 -> 25, CA-GB 21 -> 22 (Jaccard 11.1-14.9% -> 11.7-14.3%).
- Profile cosine (route A, all theses): CA-US 0.557 -> 0.556, GB-US 0.567 -> 0.566, CA-GB 0.513 -> 0.511. Equal draws:
  CA-US 0.479 -> 0.470 (0.446-0.493), CA-GB 0.428 -> 0.420, US-GB 0.464 -> 0.457. By parsed name unchanged (CA-US 0.549).

Claim 4, the periphery leans on the shared canon; France and Brazil form an axis. STANDS; route A shares slightly lower.
- Universal share by parsed name, equal draws: FR 15.6 -> 15.5%, BR 20.4 -> 20.2%, MX 20.3 -> 20.1%, against CA 8.4 ->
  8.3%, US 7.7 -> 7.6%, GB 8.7 -> 8.6%.
- The same through matched works (route A): FR 12.9 -> 12.5%, BR 19.5 -> 18.9%, MX 16.4 -> 15.6%, against CA 6.8 ->
  6.5%, US 6.0 -> 5.6%, GB 7.1 -> 6.6%. The gap between the two groups is unchanged in kind.
- France-Brazil person cosine, equal draws: by name 0.444 -> 0.448; route A 0.435 -> 0.434 (0.412-0.459); CA-US 0.47-0.48.
  All theses, route A: 0.464 -> 0.463.
- Works cosine FR-BR with the roll-up 0.280 -> 0.295 (without it 0.252 both): the canonical fixes send more Brazilian
  and French citations of translations to the same original.
- Concentration: top-100 people's share of parsed-name person citations is corpus-side and unchanged (BR 7.2%, MX 5.9%
  against 2.0-2.9%); through matched works on equal draws BR 23.2 -> 23.2%, MX 26.5 -> 27.0%, FR 11.8 -> 12.2%, against
  CA 8.2 -> 8.4%, US 6.5 -> 6.3%, GB 6.0 -> 6.1%.

Claim 5, bibliographies age slowly. STANDS for theses; the JSTOR article trend WEAKENS again.
- Corpus as printed (scholarship frame), theses 2005-09 / 2010-14 / 2015-18 / 2019-26: 17 / 18 / 19 / 20 years, under-ten
  share 27.7% -> 22.3%: unchanged (does not depend on the graph). With original years for rolled-up editions 18 / 18 /
  19 / 21, unchanged.
- Graph-matched, restricted (cited <= 2015, citing <= 2018, age >= 3), now by first publication year: 2005-09 20 -> 21,
  2010-14 21 -> 21, 2015-18 22 -> 22; on the printed year of the same pairs 19 / 19 / 20 (unchanged). Reweighted
  (inverse probability) 23 / 23 / 23 -> 24 / 24 / 24. The first year makes matched works one year older at most; the
  slope is flat in both.
- JSTOR articles, reweighted: 15 years (1970s, 1980s) -> 19 (2010s) before; 17 -> 20 now (observed 14-15 -> 18 before,
  15 -> 18 now). The 1970s-80s articles cite works whose keys carried reprint years; with first years their citations
  are older, so the rise is about three years, not four (it was seven in the morning run). Still upward, but say so with
  the interval and the change of article set, not as a firm trend.

  Tradition; Rémond; Brazilian collections: J. L. Ferreira, Bicalho, Gouvêa, De Luca, Delgado, M. de M. Ferreira, Soihet,
  Jancsó, E. da Silva; Bethell; Calhoun), which is mostly real editing (not checked one by one). The edition editors of the evening list are gone from

  Shapiro 197 -> 11, Eastman 181 -> 9, R. H. Campbell 188 -> 30, McCulloch 168 -> 12. Counting edition roles as well,
  10 of that top 500 would get half or more of their theses only through them (Engels, Schluchter, A. S. Skinner, Ewald,
  Shapiro, Campbell, McCulloch, Lerner, H. L. Gates, B. H. Edwards).
- Di Tella / Guerra. Di Tella's linked credit on Guerra's Le Mexique de l'Ancien Régime is demoted (LC author conflict):
  Mexican theses reaching him with linked credits 52 -> 0 (all theses 76 -> 13); by key-log credits 13 both. Guerra keeps
  106 theses, 64 Mexican.
- Works whose original year is later than the printed year of the translation cited (rolled-up corpus works): 1,117 ->
  227 works (2,782 -> 222 citations) by first year; by the key year it would now be 1,123. The 227 left are mostly
  corpus years misparsed from a title ('1852-1871', '1500-1700', '1815-1945').
- Veyne, Comment on écrit l'histoire. The original no longer rolls up to the English key: French and translated citations
  go to w-1189310 (first year 1971; 102 theses), but a second original key w-2325911 (2015 reprint, 71 theses) now takes
  citations too: 173 theses split over two keys (class 25, duplicate original, not merged). w-2325911 is also the top
  anachronistic work (24 theses from 1991 on).
- Anachronistic pairs (graph work dated 2+ years after the citing thesis): 2,229 pairs / 1,567 works -> 779 / 662 by first
  year (2,344 / 1,625 by the key year on the new build). The rest: the Veyne reprint key, DOIs of digitised reprints
  (Persée, Érudit, Cairn), compilations dated by their Portuguese edition.
- Duplicate person keys among the top 1,000: Thomas Bender (99 / 7 theses) and now Boyd Hilton (77 / 7).
- 'Primary source' role in the top 200: 14 -> 15 people (the role classifier is unchanged; still not usable for a

5. Claims the data did not support, now
- "Brazil ignores the international canon": still falls; Hobsbawm 28.4%, Thompson 16.6%, Le Goff 21.6%, Bourdieu 17.5%,
  Foucault 19.5% of Brazilian theses through matched works, all unchanged.
- "Sharp ageing in the 2020s": still not supported (corpus as printed 19 to 20; naive matched medians 21 to 24).
- "Articles are more canonical than theses": still not supported; 2008-2020 top-100 works take 2.24% of thesis pairs and
  1.70% of article pairs (2.2 / 1.7 before). Agreement holds: person cosine 0.749 -> 0.747; works cosine 0.578 -> 0.623
  (the canonical fixes); English only 0.82 both.
- Article concentration by decade: reweighted top-100 share 4.3-4.5% in every decade (was 4.4-4.7%); observed 3.6% (1960s)
  to 2.3% (2010s). No decline should be claimed (earlier ruling stands).
- Gini levels: still sample-size dependent.
