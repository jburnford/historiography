Canon paper: what changed when the tables were rerun on the evening build (2026-11-11)

The morning tables (kept in out-2026-11-11-morning/) were made on build/graph-check with a 37.0% thesis match rate.
The tables in out/ were made on the live build/graph of 2026-11-11 20:08-20:11 (41.96% matched). FINDINGS.md is
unchanged; this note says, claim by claim, what the new figures are and whether each claim stands, strengthens,
weakens or falls. Counts and shares only; distinct citing documents throughout; person keys are never summed.

1. What was changed in the scripts

- common.py: reads build/graph (no longer build/graph-check); scratch base in scratchpad/paper2; two switches,
  PAPER_CREDITS (keylog, the default, or linked) and PAPER_ROLLUP (1, the default, or 0); PAPER_OUT for other
  output folders. Parsed author keys with initials only ('smith|j', 'thompson|e') are now dropped from the
  parsed-name routes (corpus person level, route B, the name-route coverage in Table 1). The morning scripts did
  not do this; its effect is small (route B universal shares move by 0.0-0.3 points; corpus person top-100 shares
  move by up to 0.3 points) and is part of every old-to-new difference on those routes.
- 00_base.py: the match flag now reproduces export_cited_works.py of the evening exactly (1,280,027 rows, 41.96%):
  the three older crosswalks plus the four translation-check crosswalks and the Qwen tail (the morning script read
  only the first three, which is why it could not rebuild about 108k graph thesis edges), then LC > Crossref >
  OpenAlex (with the guards and the agreement review) > Crossref metadata, then translations recovered to their
  original. Each corpus work gets two graph keys: the rolled-up one (a translation or later edition goes to the
  original) and the one it was matched to as cited (used for the no-roll-up sensitivity). Old crosswalk keys that
  were merged away are followed to the live key through the key-log cache (read only). Compilations (corpus: works)
  are mapped but not counted as matched, as in report.json. JSTOR edges are now any source starting with 'jstor'
  (the morning rule source='jstor' would have counted the new 'jstor-matched' edges as thesis citations).
- The adaptation of the credit tables by the other agent was checked and is right: evidence 'keylog' maps to the
  morning's authored / edited, evidence 'linked' to linked_authored / linked_edited (251,069 and 2,531 rows). Note
  that 'linked' is now wider than in the morning: besides the cited-works linker it holds work-people credits
  (printed reviewers, JSTOR authors, OpenAlex co-authors), JSTOR-matched LC main entries and compilations.
- conc.py and the analyses: the credit kinds come from one function (hows) instead of the hard-coded
  ('authored','edited') in 02, 03, 04 and 08; thesis_work carries the printed year, the graph year and edition and
  translation flags. 03 adds works overlap with and without the roll-up (and on equal draws); 05 adds the printed-
  year and original-year versions; 07 adds the linked and roll-up variants and more headline numbers; 09 is new
  (translations).

Reconstruction check: the corpus-to-graph map gives 1,222,520 (thesis, work) pairs for keyed theses, 99.95% of them
also graph CITES edges; the graph holds 1,235,325, so 13,120 graph edges (older cites.csv.gz routes) are still not
rebuilt from the crosswalks (morning: about 108k). 10,068 matched citations (579 works) have an older-crosswalk key
that no longer resolves to a graph node; 646 strict theses have no diss-thesis key in the graph.

3. The five claims

Claim 1, the canon reaches far but takes little. STANDS (numbers move up a little).
- Top-100 works' share of thesis citations: corpus level 0.66% then and now (unchanged by construction); graph level
  1.63% (1.59-1.67) then, 2.04% (1.99-2.09) now. The graph value is still an upper bound.
- Theses citing at least one top-100 work: 46-51% then, 46-56% now (corpus 46.4%, graph 56.4%).
- Theses citing one of the 100 most-cited people: 68-73% then, 72-73% now (graph 72.4%, corpus 73.0%).
- Top-100 people's share of person pairs, graph: 4.4% then, 5.8% now; the top of the person ranking now holds
  Hobsbawm, Le Goff, de Certeau, Ginzburg, Burke and Koselleck far higher because Brazilian (and French) citations
  of translations now reach them.
- Random loss of matches still leaves top-100 shares unchanged (work 2.04 / 2.04 / 2.05% at 100 / 75 / 50%).

Claim 2, supervisor effect. STANDS as an effect; the ratio in the stated frame STRENGTHENS from 3.6-3.7 to 4.1-4.4,
but this is a definition effect, not a stronger finding.
- Own-supervisor citation through matched works: 46.8% (45.4-48.2) then, 48.0% now; with parsed names 56.6 then,
  56.7% now. Same 8,950 pairs from 7,741 theses.
- Topical peers (same institution and decade, 3+ shared cited works): 12.7-15.9% then, 10.9-13.8% now; ratio
  3.6-3.7 then, 4.1-4.4 (4.0-4.6) now. With more works matched, more peer theses pass the 3-shared-works bar, so
  the topical peer set is wider and less topical. The paper should state the ratio with its coupling threshold and
  should not read the rise as a stronger effect.
- Against all institution-decade peers: 21-24 then, 21-24 now (unchanged).
- By country (route A, cited outside own students): CA 46.0%, US 50.9%, GB 47.2%, FR 32.6% (125 own theses), BR
  46.2%; peer rates 0.9-3.2%.

Claim 3, Anglophone canons overlap about a fifth. STANDS (essentially unchanged).
- Top-100 people shared: CA-US 22 then, 20 now; CA-GB 21 and 21; GB-US 26 and 26 (12-15% then, 11-15% now).
- Profile cosine, all theses, route A: CA-US 0.559 to 0.557, CA-GB 0.508 to 0.513, US-GB 0.563 to 0.567; by name
  the same (CA-US 0.48 on equal draws by both routes). Equal draws: CA-US 0.48 (0.45-0.50), CA-GB 0.43, US-GB 0.46.

Claim 4, the periphery leans on the shared canon; France and Brazil form an axis. STRENGTHENS: what was true only by
parsed name is now true by matched works as well.
- Universal share (people cited in all six countries), equal draws, by name: FR 15.6%, BR 20.4%, MX 20.3% against
  CA 8.4%, US 7.7%, GB 8.7% (then 15-20% against 8-9%): unchanged.
- The same through matched works (route A): FR 8.2 to 12.9%, BR 5.3 to 19.5%, MX 8.3 to 16.4%, against CA 4.9 to
  6.8%, US 4.5 to 6.0%, GB 5.1 to 7.1%. Route A now tells the same story as the name route.
- France-Brazil cosine, equal draws: by name 0.45 (0.42-0.47) then, 0.44 (0.42-0.47) now; route A 0.15 then,
  0.44 (0.40-0.48) now. Canada-US 0.48 by both routes. The France-Brazil axis is now as close as Canada-US by
  both routes.
- Concentration: top-100 people take 6.9% (BR) and 5.8% (MX) of parsed-name person citations then, 7.2% and 5.9%
  now, against 1.7-2.8% then and 2.0-2.9% elsewhere now. By matched works the gap is the same shape (BR 23%, MX
  26%, against 6-12%).
- Cross-national overlap on works versus authors. Works overlap far less than authors, and the roll-up narrows the
  gap only a little. Works cosine (all theses): FR-BR 0.252 without roll-up, 0.280 with it (person route A 0.464);
  US-FR 0.169 to 0.199; CA-FR 0.215 to 0.238; US-BR 0.148 to 0.172; CA-US 0.461 to 0.464 and GB-US 0.423 to 0.425
  (person 0.56-0.57). The roll-up adds 10-30% relative to cross-language work overlap and almost nothing within
  English. Countries share authors much more than they share particular books: Brazil cites Le Goff's Storia e
  memoria and Chartier's A historia cultural (a compilation, its own node), the US cites Imagined Communities.

Claim 5, bibliographies age slowly. STANDS for theses. Which year: NAIVE and RESTRICTED use the graph work's year,
which with the roll-up is the original's key year (itself sometimes a reprint year); CORPUS uses the year printed in
the bibliography (the edition cited). Both are now shown, and a corpus version that replaces the printed year by
the original's graph year for rolled-up editions (only when that year is not later than the printed one).
- Median age, corpus as printed, theses 2005-09 / 2010-14 / 2015-18 / 2019-26: 17 / 18 / 19 / 20 then and now;
  under-ten share 27.7% to 22.3% then and now (the corpus side does not depend on matching).
- With original years for rolled-up editions: 18 / 18 / 19 / 21; under ten 27.2% to 22.2%. The slow ageing holds.
- Graph-matched, restricted (cited <= 2015, citing <= 2018, age >= 3): graph year 20 / 21 / 22 (2005-09, 2010-14,
  2015-18; was 20 / 20 / 21); printed year for the same pairs 19 / 19 / 20. The graph year now runs one or two
  years older than the printed year because rolled-up translations carry the original's date.
- Brazil (theses 2010-18, corpus, cited <= 2015): 16 printed, 17 with original years; others unchanged at 20-21.
  The rolled-up citations alone are much older than their printed dates: Brazil 20 printed against 35 original;
  US 27 against 39; GB 30 against 43; FR 28 against 42.
- JSTOR articles, reweighted: 12 years (1970s-80s) to 19 (2010s) then; 15 to 19 now (observed 14 to 18, was
  12-13 to 17). The JSTOR article set changed (jstor-matched edges roughly double the pairs: 817k to 1,317k), so
  the article trend WEAKENS: still upward, but by 4 years, not 7.

4. Claims the data did not support

- "Brazil ignores the international canon." FALLS further, and the morning explanation needs a correction. Through
  matched works Hobsbawm now reaches 28.4% of Brazilian theses (0.8% in the morning; 31.9% by name), Thompson
  16.6% (0.5%; 18.2%), Le Goff 21.6% (0.6%; 27.0%), Bourdieu 17.5% (1.8%; 29.1%), Foucault 19.5% (13.5%;
  24.7%). Most of the morning gap was not the catalogue: the morning base script left out the four translation-
  check crosswalks and the Qwen tail that already fed the graph. Without them, Hobsbawm, Le Goff, Thompson and
  Bourdieu reach only 1.4%, 1.0%, 0.5% and 1.8% of Brazilian theses; with them 27.8%, 21.3%, 16.4% and 16.7%. The
  roll-up itself matters for Le Goff (7.9% without it, 21.6% with it), little for the others (Hobsbawm 28.1 to
  28.4%). Portuguese editions counted for the original author are the reason: 6.0% of all Brazilian citations
  and 16.9% of the matched ones go to an identified translation, and 90% of Brazilian theses cite at least one.
  The point for the paper still holds in a weaker form: a cross-national claim on matched works is only as good as
  the translation links behind it.
- "Sharp ageing in the 2020s." Still not supported: matched-only medians go 21 to 24 (was 19 to 23); the corpus as
  printed gives 19 to 20 (20 to 21 with original years).
- Gini and top-1% levels. Still move with sample size (work Gini 0.49 to 0.40 under a random 50% loss).
- Article concentration by decade (07-article-decade-reweighted.csv, not one of the five claims): observed top-100
  share falls from 3.7% (1960s) to 2.3% (2010s) (morning 5.9% to 3.0%), but reweighted by the thesis match rate it is
  now flat at 4.4-4.7% (morning 6.7% falling to 4.3%). A decline in article canon concentration should not be claimed.
- "Articles are more canonical than theses." Still not supported, and now the sign flips: 2008-2020, the top-100
  works take 2.2% of thesis pairs and 1.7% of article pairs (1.8 and 2.4% in the morning). Agreement holds: person
  cosine 0.75 (0.77 then), top-100 overlap 0.27 (0.30); English only 0.82 (0.82).
- "Historians only" results. Still not supported: 14 of the top-200 people are labelled 'primary source' (18 then);
  Brazilian scholars and Marx/Engels among them.

7. Things in the new graph that look wrong from the analysis side

- Translators, editors and co-authors credited with the original work. The edition-editor problem (class 15)
  persists in the translated layer: Wolfgang Schluchter (303 theses, 296 through EDITED; Weber's editor), Francois
  Ewald (218, 203 EDITED; Foucault's lectures), Max Lerner, Andrew Skinner, David McLellan, Ian Shapiro, Max Eastman.
  26 of the top-500 people get half or more of their theses through EDITED credits (37 in the morning). New with the
  linked credits: Torcuato Di Tella is credited (work-people:author, from an OpenAlex record) with Guerra's Le
  Mexique de l'Ancien Regime, so he shows 52 Mexican theses, all via that one work. Count people by authored credits
  only, and audit work-people author credits on books.
- Original-year contamination. 1,117 of the 10,667 rolled-up corpus works point to an original whose key year is
  later than the translation's printed year (Weber's Protestant Ethic key 1934 against a 1930 English translation;
  Burke's French Historical Revolution 2015 against Portuguese editions of 1991 and 1997; Il principe 1891; Thomas's
  Man and the Natural World 1996). Graph-year ages are unreliable for these; the 05 original-year version ignores
  any graph year later than the printed one.
- Anachronistic matches rose from 1,621 to 2,229 pairs (0.18% of graph pairs): reprint-year work keys (The Great
  Plains 2022, The Sense of Power 2013) and Crossref-metadata matches to reprint DOIs (239 rows: Althusser's
  Ideology and Ideological State Apparatuses 2024, Guha's essay 2022). Also a review keyed as a book: "The Houses
  of History (Anna Green and Kathleen Troup)" (2016, cited by theses from 2004).
- An original matched to its translation's key: Veyne's Comment on ecrit l'histoire (68 French citations) goes to
  the key of the English Writing History (1984), and the roll-up does not reverse this direction.
- Double count in report.json (see Table 1): compilation rows also matched by an older crosswalk are added twice in
  rate_with_compilations (42.07% should be 41.98%). Edge counting in the graph itself is clean: thesis-edition edges
  are flagged edition = false when the thesis also cites the original directly, and no (thesis, work) pair is
  counted twice in the tables, which count distinct documents.
- Duplicate person keys among the top 1,000: one group left (Thomas Bender, 99 and 7 theses).

8. Not verified

- The match flag is checked against report.json to the row, but the per-work graph key for the 2026-11-10/11
  crosswalks is rebuilt from identifiers, not read from the build; 13,120 graph edges and 10,068 matched citations
  are not reproduced (section 1).
- The translation layer's precision was not re-audited here (its own audit samples are in
  data/identifiers/cited-works-translations-2026-11-11/); the translation shares are lower bounds.
- The work-people linked credits (JSTOR authors, reviewers, OpenAlex co-authors) were not audited; the Di Tella case
  shows at least one wrong book credit.
- build/graph was being rebuilt by another session during the day; these tables are from the files written
  20:08-20:11. A later build will move the figures.
