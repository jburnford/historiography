# Dedicated historian relationship reviews

Use one researcher per historian, with an independent reviewer checking the
actual evidence and integration. Gareth Stedman Jones is the first dedicated
review under this protocol. The earlier all-entry pass was a semantic screen;
it does not substitute for the fresh source work required here.

Flag historians with fewer than six relationships OR fewer than three distinct
named person neighbors. The pinned 1.194 queue flags 158 of 203 historians: 132
have fewer than six total relationships and 155 fewer than three people. These
are research priorities, not 158 demonstrated defects. Opposite directions and
multiple claims with one person count as one neighbor; group routes remain
intellectually meaningful but do not count as personal breadth.

Keep dossier status separate from live degree counts. A historian does not become
reviewed merely because another person’s additions push them above a cutoff;
the pinned queue and explicit research outcomes preserve that distinction.

Higher-degree entries can override the numerical screen when primary reading
is thin, named relationships remain on group endpoints, or a specific scholarly
exchange is missing. The Scott example shows that useful transmission and
criticism can coexist; neither a mandatory balance nor an edge quota follows.

For each person, the dedicated researcher must:

1. Inspect every current incoming/outgoing claim, its sources, direction,
   coauthors and mixed-attribution limits.
2. Identify named debts, responses, collaborative arguments and methodological
   reception in the biography and existing source notes. Select actual works
   and exchanges to investigate, rather than scan for name occurrences alone.
3. Read fresh selected primary passages for each proposed claim; record exact
   locators, original/reprint/translation dates, speaker and subject identity,
   coauthor credit, access limitations and what remains unread. If the original
   is inaccessible, a substantive named scholarly reader may support an explicitly
   attributed relationship with exact reader locators and unread-original limits.
   Do not label that mediated route a fresh reading of the original; broader
   unsupported claims remain held.
4. Compare proposals with every relevant current edge, including group routes;
   distinguish a new argument from a repaired/projection route.
5. Record each investigated lead as a verified omission, already represented,
   held for missing endpoint, held for unread/uncertain evidence, or searched
   without a verified omission. Missing endpoints do not create automatic bios.
6. Submit the bounded dossier to independent passage-level and coverage review.
   The reviewer must challenge omitted named exchanges and user-supplied leads,
   as well as verify proposed additions; uninvestigated leads remain explicit.
   One researcher owns the dossier, with targeted bibliography or argument
   fact-checks feeding that record where needed. The coordinator merges exact
   deltas, preserves prior qualifications and accepted packets, verifies the
   build and publishes reviewed batches.

Completion means the specified research protocol was completed with explicit
limits. It does not certify an exhaustive intellectual network. Degree is a
triage signal, not historical importance or a target to pad toward. Research
and implementation keep separate status. OpenAlex remains paused.
