# Dissertation canon: missing-content review

Review date: **2026-10-24**. Teaching graph: **revision 1.164**. Corpus:
**frozen 2026-10-23 release**, with 7,107 analysis dissertations, including
6,794 with years. This is a local research handoff; no graph or public assets
have been changed.

The strongest gaps concern arguments, methods and debates that dissertations
use but the teaching graph does not yet explain. Some warrant new identities
or entries; others belong in existing fields. Missing standalone biographies
are not a sufficient reason to add content.

The screen examined **3,037 author keys and 1,633 work IDs**, using thresholds
of 30 dissertations per author key and 20 per work, plus the reviewed public
exceptions. These are discovery leads, not thousands of verified omissions.
The human reviews below select historiographical interventions, check existing
rosters and strands, and consult publisher, library or institutional evidence.
They do not exhaust every work in the corpus.

## Deeper treatment within existing coverage

Ricœur is already indexed and appears through Chartier/White reception. His
own narrative, historical epistemology and memory arguments need direct
treatment: *Temps et récit* has **89** dated dissertations in its exact corpus
record. The two French *Memory, History, Forgetting* records have **33** and
**27**, and the English record **17**; none is summed.

Goffman's self-presentation and institutional analysis, de Certeau's
historiographical operation, Williams's keywords and rural/urban culture,
Sewell's eventful temporality, Butler's materialization, and Bourdieu's forms
of capital are specific work/method expansions. Existing coverage of their
other concepts remains valid. Cooper's *Colonialism in Question* (**107**)
and Roediger's *The Wages of Whiteness* (**120**) similarly need more than
their present roster-level treatment. Halbwachs and Bakhtin have narrower
work/edition gaps; Agamben and Arendt require carefully qualified conceptual
research, including criticism and distinct publication/edition histories.

Other bounded leads include Hartog's regimes of historicity (**59**), Moyn's
human-rights periodization in *The Last Utopia* (**44**), deindustrialization,
and historical thinking/didactics. Hunt's existing 2007 rights reception
already includes Moyn/Bell criticism; the gap concerns a further argument,
not an absence of criticism. Corbin's sensory history is another method lead.

The deindustrialization proposal concerns a Cowie/Harvey comparison. Existing
Canadian working-class coverage already includes Steven High's *Industrial
Sunset* and a deindustrialization strand; urban history includes Sugrue's
industrial-change strand. Those are starting points for deeper treatment.

## Existing individual entries: eight distinct work gaps

The refreshed 19-flag list resolves into **eight missing works or collections,
nine already-represented translations/editions, and two unresolved records**.
The eight research leads are:

- Skinner, *Visions of Politics* (2002, three-volume collection).
- Levi, “On Microhistory” (1991 chapter; later editions remain distinct).
- Pocock, *Virtue, Commerce, and History* (1985 collection), “British History:
  A Plea for a New Subject” (1975 article), and *Politics, Language and Time*
  (1971 collection).
- Koselleck, *The Practice of Conceptual History* (2002 translated collection)
  and *L'expérience de l'histoire* (1997 French edited collection).
- Ginzburg **and Carlo Poni**, “La micro-histoire” (1981 partial translation
  of their joint 1979 Italian essay).

These are bibliographic gaps, not eight wholly missing concepts. Collections
can contain essays already covered at their original dates. Research must
retain coauthors, translators, editors and original/component dates. The
Swedish Koselleck selection needs a contents comparison; the old truncated
Foucault sexuality-volume record still cannot be assigned safely. See the
[19 explicit decisions](individual-work-review.json).

## Coverage and uncertainty to retain

- Canadian environmental, labour and gender coverage already contains
  substantive work on Loo, Piper, Sandlos, Sangster and related scholars.
  Their lack of individual nodes is not proof of missing content.
- The archival turn, Stoler, Hartman, Fuentes, material culture and object
  biography already have coverage. Reviewers excluded these blanket gaps.
- The earlier **148 work-review decisions** remain frozen. The refreshed
  individual-entry screen has **19** flags after applying that ledger; flags
  include translations and the same unresolved truncated Foucault volume.
  They are not 19 newly established omissions.
- Release author totals include **all credited authorship positions**, including
  undated dissertations. Public person charts count **first-author work keys
  in dated dissertations**. For Scott these are 464 and 455 respectively;
  their difference cannot be attributed solely to missing years.
- One name key can mix identities. `bell|catherine e` combines the ritual
  theorist with a First Nations law scholar; its 32-dissertation person total
  is not usable as a single-scholar claim. Initials, corporate authors and
  parser tokens also appear high in the automated list.
- Corpus years can describe translations, editions or parsed mistakes.
  Catalogue descriptions and publisher summaries support research leads,
  not whole-book reading or passage-level relationship claims.
- The regional and cohort samples differ. France is heavily represented
  within Europe; 2026 is partial. Region/cohort counts are descriptive context,
  not controlled evidence of a scholarly turn. Citation presence does not
  distinguish methodological use from factual reference, critique or primary use.
  Frequency cutoffs also inherit repository and language coverage; they cannot
  decide the relative importance of traditions underrepresented in this corpus.
