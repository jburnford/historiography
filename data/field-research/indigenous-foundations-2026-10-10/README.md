# Foundations and collaborations in academic Indigenous history

Academic Indigenous history brings together community knowledge, historical writing,
archival inquiry and varied scholarly relationships. The local graph now makes two
specific collaborations visible alongside related labour, institutional, geographical
and settler-colonial scholarship. These examples establish particular contributions;
they do not establish a universal sequence of development or rank the people involved.

## Stó:lō historical practice

The field-school programme combines community priorities and cultural teaching with
university research and instruction. Its [institutional history](https://hcmc.uvic.ca/project/stolo/index.html)
dates the first class to **1998**. The published account by Carlson, Lutz and Schaepe
describes **planning in 1997**, including consultation with chiefs, community members,
McHalsie, Schaepe and archivist David Smith. The two dates represent different events.
[Turning the Page, p. 4](https://artsandscience.usask.ca/keithcarlson/Publications/2-chapters/Turning%20the%20Page.PDF).

The shared approach credits **Naxaxalhts’i / Albert “Sonny” McHalsie**, **David M.
Schaepe**, **David Smith**, **Keith Thor Carlson** and **John Lutz**. It also identifies
the participation of elders, hosts and students. McHalsie’s institutional profile
records his teaching and mentoring from 1998; he appears as a historical educator.
[UVic Ring, June 2011, p. 6](https://www.uvic.ca/communicationsmarketing/assets/docs/ring/archives/2011/ring-june2011-web.pdf).

Later publications remain later. The community’s publication list dates the historical
atlas to **2001**, while **Towards a New Ethnohistory** appeared in **2018**, with
Carlson, Lutz, Schaepe and McHalsie credited as editors. Neither book is assigned to
1998 or admitted as an additional post-2000 intervention in this batch.
[Community publication list](https://www.srrmcentre.com/publications),
[University of Manitoba Press](https://uofmpress.ca/books/towards-a-new-ethnohistory).

## Further regional work

The Australian and Aotearoa New Zealand candidates discussed earlier remain research
leads for a comparable inquiry into methods and collaborations: Rowley, Reynolds,
Ryan, Attwood, Binney, Orange, Belich and Salmond. A dated book and shared topic do
not alone establish how a scholar helped incorporate Indigenous methods. Primary
prefaces, acknowledgements, coauthor credits and accounts by community participants
should determine the next additions. Existing Indigenous regional contributors remain
represented independently.

A more specific Aotearoa lead is **Ngā Mōrehu (1986)**. The publisher names Binney
and Gillian Chaplin and eight Māori narrators: Heni Brown, Reremoana Koopu, Maaka
Jones, Hei Ariki Algie, Heni Sunderland, Miria Rua, Putiputi Onekawa and Te Akakura
Rua. The National Library confirms 1986 as first publication, separately from the
1996 and 2011 editions. These credits support a collaboration inquiry; the book’s
methodological introduction remains unread. [Publisher](https://www.bwb.co.nz/books/nga-morehu),
[National Library](https://natlib.govt.nz/records/21697539). Reynolds’s publisher
confirms documentary and oral evidence in *The Other Side of the Frontier* (first
published 1981), but does not establish an equivalent community collaboration.
[UNSW Press](https://unsw.press/books/the-other-side-of-the-frontier/).
The [regional research ledger](regional-research-leads.json) retains these bounded
findings and required checks outside the accepted graph.
