# Reading scope for the field separation

Checked 18 September 2026, local date. These are editorial reading notes, not archived copies of the primary texts. No new third-party PDF is added to repository/browser assets. Earlier source and citation records retain their existing limits.

**Crenshaw, 1989:** [primary article](https://ideas.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Crenshaw-1989.pdf), printed pp. 139–140. The opening centres Black women and criticizes analyses treating race and gender as mutually exclusive. It distinguishes intersectional experience from an additive account of separate discriminations and acknowledges earlier Black feminist work. This supports a distinct intersectional approach and its conceptual connection to gender/racial inquiry. It does not establish borrowing from Omi and Winant. No contemporary legal advice or complete case analysis is derived.

**Omi and Winant:** [course extract](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/bodies/media/Omi%20and%20Winant,%20Racial%20Formations.pdf), PDF pp. 1–3, particularly “Race as a Social Concept” and the definition of racial formation. The text treats racial meanings as historically variable and constituted through social, political and economic relations. This supports the separate programme and a comparison with intersectional questions; it does not make them identical. The course heading labels the extract second edition but its editor attribution and pagination are not independently authenticated. The existing record distinguishes original 1986 from revised 1994. The checked formulation is recorded against that later witness, not silently assigned to the original edition. Comparative regional assertions remain the authors’ claims.

**Cathy J. Cohen, 1997:** [primary scan](https://blackwomenintheblackfreedomstruggle.voices.wooster.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/210/2019/02/Cathy-J-Cohen_Punks-bulldaggers.pdf), printed pp. 437–442; raster OCR read, p. 441 also visually inspected. Cohen critiques queer politics organised through a simple heterosexual/queer division and explicitly calls for analysis of intersecting systems of oppression. On p. 441 she names Crenshaw and other Black feminist writers and quotes the Combahee River Collective. This supports a bounded critique within queer politics and named intellectual use. It does not establish a consensus of queer historians or identify the exact Crenshaw publication from the name alone.
