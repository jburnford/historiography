# Whole-map editorial review — 10 September 2026

The review addresses a modeling failure: a field’s incidental cross-links were being presented more prominently than its central historians. Kuhn was already in the data, but his unclassified connection did not enter the directional map. The same pattern obscured the Annales historians and the British Marxist historians. Adding isolated names would not correct that display rule.

Editorial revision **1.9**, schema **1.4**, contains **109 graph entries, 66 group rosters, 228 shared people, 277 contextual selections, 331 relationships, 165 bibliography records, and 13 pathways**. There are **73 internal approaches across 22 group entries**. Every group now exposes its entire selected roster above its connections. Focused maps and lists display every matching recorded relationship without pagination.

## What was reviewed

- Every group: core-person selection, roles, category, and scope. Every individual entry remains discoverable through its shared identity, works, and connections. The coverage matrix below records all 66 groups; the companion audit includes all 109 entries.
- Every relationship: explicit direction or comparison status and reference availability. The 155 unclassified relationships in revision 1.7 received individual editorial classifications across revisions 1.8 and 1.9, with bounded evidence notes. Similarity alone is not treated as influence.
- Broad labels: selected fields now contain explicit approaches with people, works, methodological distinctions, and sources. These are teaching distinctions, not new graph nodes, exclusive memberships, or inferred communities.
- Visibility: browser checks compare every focused neighborhood with the actual edge set and each core-person list with the full selected roster. This establishes that records are displayed, not that the historical selection is exhaustive.

## Substantive corrections

### History of science

Kuhn is visible in the core roster and as an incoming contribution through `edge_131`. The selection also includes Sarton, Koyré, Merton, Shapin, and Schaffer. The four approaches distinguish institutional formation, intellectual and conceptual history, Kuhn’s historical account of scientific change, and social histories of knowledge. Yates and Foucault retain qualified additional relationships. They do not substitute for the field’s historians. The field is older than Kuhn and STS. See [Kuhn’s book](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/S/bo13179781.html) and [Poskett’s historiographical review](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0018246X18000195).

### Annales and Marxist social history

Annales now has 20 recorded connections: 10 incoming, 5 outgoing, and 5 comparisons. Bloch, Febvre, Braudel, Labrousse, and Chartier have individual contribution links. The roster also identifies Simiand, Pirenne, Berr, Le Goff, and Le Roy Ladurie with distinct roles. Documentary continuity and environmental affinities are comparisons where the evidence does not justify a genealogy. Huizinga’s reception is scoped to Febvre; Braudel’s downstream world-systems connection is scoped to Wallerstein. These are not claims about every historian under either umbrella. See [Burke’s Annales review](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0020859000010063), [Braudel’s 1958 essay](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ahess_0395-2649_1958_num_13_4_2781?pageId=T1_729), and [Chartier’s intervention](https://doi.org/10.3406/ahess.1989.283667).

Marxist social history now has 27 connections: 13 incoming, 12 outgoing, and 2 comparisons. Its core roster includes E. P. Thompson, Dorothy Thompson, Hobsbawm, Hill, Dobb, Hilton, and Torr. The new individual links expose contributions that were previously buried in prose. Tawney is an external interlocutor, not silently made a member of the Communist Party Historians’ Group. Dobb’s transition debates and Torr’s collective historical work have support in [Hobsbawm’s participant account](https://www.versobooks.com/blogs/news/the-historians-group-of-the-communist-party); Tawney’s own introduction identifies debts to Ashley and Weber in [Religion and the Rise of Capitalism](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/71223/71223-h/71223-h.htm).

Thompson remains part of an early cultural reorientation within Marxist historical work. Contributions from Thompson and British Marxist history to gender history remain distinct from feminist critiques of their limits. Neither “gender historians” nor “the cultural turn” denotes a uniform position. Revision 1.7’s source qualifications and all four contribution/critique links are preserved.

### Economic history and other broad entries

Economic history has seven approaches: historical economics and disciplinary institutions; British economic and social history; French serial and conjunctural history; capitalist development and class relations; cliometrics; institutional change; slavery, empire, and unequal development. Labels name mechanisms and people where possible. For example, a Labrousse connection concerns series, conjunctures, and social structures; it is not a generic claim that economic history produced Annales. [North’s 1993 lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/1993/north/lecture/) and [Fogel’s lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/fogel-lecture.pdf) help distinguish quantitative and institutional projects.

Other subdivisions separate dependency from world-systems analysis; Italian microhistory from Alltagsgeschichte; gender from racial formation and intersectionality; world, connected, and comparative global histories; and different linguistic-turn, Frankfurt, and practice-theory projects. Each subdivision carries a focus statement and contextual references.

Thin rosters were expanded beyond the three examples raised by the user. Modernization now distinguishes Rostow, Lerner, Parsons, and Lipset. Indigenous history includes Walker’s Māori historical interpretation and O’Brien’s study of land and identity alongside Smith’s decolonizing methods. Black history includes Franklin and Hine alongside its earlier independent scholarship. Oral history adds Nevins’s institutional route; new social history adds Gutman, Hobsbawm, Tilly, and Wrigley. These are situated selections, not exhaustive national or disciplinary canons. Sources include [Parsons’s abstract](https://www.d.umn.edu/cla/faculty/jhamlin/4111/Readings/ParsonsUniversals.htm), [O’Brien’s bibliography](https://cla.umn.edu/about/directory/profile/obrie002), [Walker’s institutional account](https://www.auckland.ac.nz/en/news/2023/09/08/Life-and-legacy-of-Ranginui-Walker.html), [Franklin’s Duke retrospective](https://history.duke.edu/news/75-years-after-publishing-his-magnum-opus-john-hope-franklin-still-inspiration), [Hine’s bibliography](https://blackstudies.northwestern.edu/people/emeritus-faculty/darlene-clark-hine.html), and [Columbia’s account of Nevins](https://c250.columbia.edu/c250_celebrates/remarkable_columbians/allan_nevins.html).

## Limits and remaining source work

**This is a completed editorial and display review of the current graph, not a claim that every relationship has been independently verified against historical passages.** Bibliography coverage remains uneven. Among 165 sources, 51 have scoped supporting-page checks, 25 have bibliographic metadata checks, 37 are marked not checked, and 52 have no recorded verification status. A checked page may support only a title, institutional role, or one part of an interpretation. Source counts cannot be converted into a count of verified edges.

The companion [machine-readable audit](feedback/editorial-review-2026-09-10.json) records every node’s roster, scope, approaches, and connection counts, and every edge’s evidence note and source-status notes. It preserves specific limits instead of assigning a blanket “verified” badge.

Further editorial work should prioritize passage-level reception evidence for inherited directional claims; contextual work references for the 70 roster selections that still lack a work string; and regional or methodological depth beyond the present selections. These are substantive content tasks. The browser tests cannot establish that the canon is complete. The current historical scope remains 1920–2000 with earlier roots; later retrospective sources do not extend the mapped period. OpenAlex remains paused.
