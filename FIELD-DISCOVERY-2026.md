# Field discovery for the 2026 extension

Prepared 2026-09-18 in response to the user's explicit field list. **Research queue and planning audit, not accepted graph additions.** This is an independent discovery track alongside review of the existing 73 topics. The candidate universe must be able to grow beyond both those topics and this initial list.

## How to read the queue

Current representation was checked against entry labels, descriptions and relevant strands in `historiography-1920-2000.json` (revision 1.118). A thematic mention is not adequate field coverage. “No dedicated entry” describes this graph, not the existence or importance of scholarship. Related entries below are research starting points, not accepted influence edges. This is not a complete audit of the separate journal catalogue.

Candidates can resolve to a new field, interdisciplinary programme, method, debate, addressable strand, extension of an existing entry, or documented deferral. A field may need a separate entry even if a small strand already exists. Conversely, an established entry should not be duplicated merely because its label or scope needs revision. None of these subjects is assumed to have originated after 2000.

## Checked starting sources and chronological cautions

These are source leads checked for the stated limited purpose. They do not certify every candidate's historical significance or support all possible edges.

- **Digital history and web archives:** Ian Milligan's [research description](https://www.ianmilligan.ca/research/) identifies web archives and born-digital evidence as central to his work. His 2022 [The Transformation of Historical Research in the Digital Age](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/transformation-of-historical-research-in-the-digital-age/30DFBEAA3B753370946B7A98045CFEF4) distinguishes forms of digital historical practice and discusses earlier quantitative and computational traditions. Use this to formulate specific continuity/discontinuity claims, and seek other participants' positions rather than settle the boundary by editorial fiat.
- **History of knowledge:** Burke's *A Social History of Knowledge: From Gutenberg to Diderot* is dated October 2000 by its [publisher record](https://www.wiley-vch.de/de/fachgebiete/geistes-und-sozialwissenschaften/social-history-of-knowledge-978-0-7456-2485-3). This is a work milestone, not a field origin. Chicago's [coverage record](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/inst/content-coverage) dates *KNOW* from spring 2017 to fall 2024, and its [journal description](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/journals/know/about) explicitly records cessation in 2024. The [Journal for the History of Knowledge archive](https://journalhistoryknowledge.org/issue/archive/) begins with volume 1 (2020); its inaugural [“What is the History of Knowledge?”](https://journalhistoryknowledge.org/article/view/11156) is a programme source to examine.
- **Agnotology:** Stanford's [record of the Proctor and Schiebinger volume](https://history.stanford.edu/publications/agnotology-making-and-unmaking-ignorance) confirms *Agnotology: The Making and Unmaking of Ignorance* (2008). Distinguish the book's publication from earlier terminology and research.
- **History of the humanities:** The publisher's [launch announcement](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/journals/hoh/pr/140915) dates the journal's first issue to spring 2016 and identifies Bod's earlier synthesis. The inaugural [“A New Field: History of Humanities”](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/685056), by Bod, Kursell, Maat and Weststeijn, offers an explicit claim of field formation. Treat that claim as attributable evidence and investigate its reception.
- **History of information:** The [companion's Harvard-hosted bibliography](https://infohist.fas.harvard.edu/) identifies *Information: A Historical Companion* (2021) and supplies a broader bibliography. Follow this outward; a single reference book does not define the entire field's boundary.
- **Memory studies:** The publisher's [first issue](https://journals.sagepub.com/toc/mssa/1/1) confirms January 2008 and contains both programme statements and debate over consolidation. Connect this later institutional development to the existing field, including Nora, without reducing its origins to one person or journal.

## An open-ended discovery procedure

1. **Seed broadly.** Start with every row above and the existing graph, but also scan recent historiographical surveys, handbook contents, journal launch editorials and special issues, learned societies, research centres and conference programmes. Wikipedia field pages, bibliographies and relevant-language editions supply another stream. Journals and conferences provide leads; their existence alone is not acceptance evidence.
2. **Extract proposed formations, not just people.** For each source, record named fields, methods, debates and alternative labels, including programmes that do not fit any existing graph entry. Save the exact source, date, passage and discovery route. Preserve original-language terms and distinguish translation from asserted equivalence.
3. **Produce a candidate dossier.** Record scope, research object, methods, older roots, dated consolidation claims, representative works, multiple contributors, venues, critiques, neighbouring formations, regional/language differences, and missing evidence. Explicitly state why current entries are inadequate or how they could be extended.
4. **Evaluate scholarly substance.** Look for a coherent research programme and sustained scholarship or explicit debate. Independent surveys and reception help establish significance. A journal, Wikipedia article, English-language label or professional association is helpful evidence but never a mandatory gate. Emerging programmes can be recorded with their limited reception made explicit.
5. **Resolve representation.** Recommend new entry, addressable strand, debated relationship, extension, alias reconciliation or deferral. Record the reason and evidence. Permit overlapping programmes and cross-field connections; do not force a unique parent. Distinguish a historical actor's claim to found a field from the editor's acceptance of that claim.
6. **Expand and challenge the queue.** Follow references and criticism into additional candidates. In each thematic batch, deliberately search for regional/original-language formations absent from the English-language seed vocabulary. An LLM's suggested field remains a lead until supported by an identifiable source.
7. **Audit outcomes.** Track all seeds and subsequent discoveries, their dispositions and unresolved questions. Report newly discovered candidates separately from reviewed existing topics. Revisit candidates whose evidence is sparse because of access or language; absence from Wikipedia must not become rejection.

## Required candidate dossier fields

`candidate_id` (research queue only), preferred label, aliases/language, proposed kind, discovery source and date, scope, related existing IDs, existing representation, reason for separate treatment, works, contributors and roles, chronology with date meanings, institutions/venues, proposed claims and passage citations, objections/competing boundary definitions, regional/language gaps, editorial disposition and rationale, next research action.

Do not assign production graph IDs before identity and representation decisions. Do not promote a venue launch year to field birth, a first keyword match to adequate coverage, or a suggested neighbour to an accepted relationship.

## Pilot and completion changes

Add **history of knowledge and its neighbouring programmes** as a fourth pilot alongside Great Divergence, Tackett/Chinese history, and digital history/web history. This tests discovery of a missing field, distinctions among knowledge/information/ignorance/humanities, and institutional milestones with independent chronologies. The digital pilot must keep digital history, computational history, digital humanities, HGIS and web history distinguishable while researching their relationships.

Completion now requires both an explicit outcome for all existing topics and a documented disposition for every user-supplied seed, plus an independent discovery pass beyond the seed list. A deferred candidate needs an evidence gap and next step; it must not disappear into a generic parent entry. These requirements make the plan extensible without claiming that every possible field has been found.
