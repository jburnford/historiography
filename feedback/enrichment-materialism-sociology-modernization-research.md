# Historical materialism, interpretive sociology and modernization research

2026-09-12; revision 1.49. Data only; no frontend design, visual review, deployment or OpenAlex.

The readings distinguish Marx’s 1859 propositions from his 1877 scope qualification; Engels’s interactive causation from abandoning determination; Weber’s meaningful interpretation from an untested account of motives; and distinct modernization explanations. Source scopes below are the reading record, not a claim that whole books or historical laws were verified. Earlier research remains in its own batch records.

## marx_1859_preface

Karl Marx, preface to A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy (1859), reproduced from Progress Publishers 1977.

https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1859/critique-pol-economy/preface.htm

Read complete primary preface body. Supports Marx’s account of forces/relations, forms in which people fight out conflicts, critical reexamination of Hegel and collaboration with Engels. The strong developmental propositions are attributed theoretical claims. Later website endnotes about an Asiatic mode or Engels’s interpretation are not adopted as Marx’s 1859 text; original edition uncollated.

## marx_russia_letter

Karl Marx, letter to the editorial board of Otechestvenniye Zapiski (November 1877), English reproduction.

https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/11/russia.htm

Read complete primary letter body. Marx restricts Capital’s account of expropriation to Western Europe and rejects a universal historical passport; the comparison with ancient Rome illustrates his argument, not independently verified Roman history. Russia’s future remains conditional. Website credits the 1968 Selected Correspondence translation to Torr, with anomalous publication metadata and an anachronistic French-edition date; those details are not certified. French manuscript and critical-edition translation remain uncollated.

## engels_bloch_primary

Friedrich Engels to Joseph Bloch, 21–22 September 1890, reproduced English translation in The New International 1.3 (1934), 81–85.

https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1890/letters/90_09_21a.htm

Read complete letter body; date spans heading September 21 and concluding September 22. Host credits Sidney Hook’s translation with journal emendations. Point II retains economic determination in the last instance while admitting political, legal and theoretical effects and an earlier imbalance of emphasis. His self-assessment and Point I kinship generalizations are not independent historical findings. German text, translation collation and website first-publication claim unchecked.

## lukacs_orthodox_primary

Georg / György Lukács, “What is Orthodox Marxism?” (dated March 1919), in History and Class Consciousness (1923), English transcription.

https://www.marxists.org/archive/lukacs/works/history/orthodox.htm

Read opening and complete sections 1–2; section 3 partly read, sections 4–5 unread. Method, theory/practice and historically conditioned facts support the bounded interpretation; the essay also advances strong dialectical truth claims and criticizes Engels, not an unrestricted methodological pluralism. Archive contents credits Rodney Livingstone but dates Merlin 1967; Indiana catalogue https://iucat.iu.edu/iuk/3251179 and WorldCat https://search.worldcat.org/it/title/143706 corroborate 1971 English editions. Exact transcription/original collation remains open.

## luxemburg_accumulation_primary

Rosa Luxemburg, The Accumulation of Capital (1913), chapter 31, “Protective Tariffs and Accumulation,” translated by Agnes Schwarzschild (1951).

https://www.marxists.org/archive/luxemburg/1913/accumulation-capital/ch31.htm

Read complete chapter 31, including its disagreement with Engels in note 4; archive contents identifies the 1951 Routledge and Kegan Paul source and Schwarzschild translation. Supports Luxemburg’s argument that violence and relations with noncapitalist settings accompany accumulation, not just its origins. Collapse predictions and historical examples are her claims, not verified conclusions. Chapters 26 and 32 were only encountered in indexing/opening extracts and are not counted as read; original print uncollated.

## lenin_imperialism_preface

https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1916/imp-hsc/pref02.htm

Read the entire reproduced 1920 preface sections I–V and following book-introduction paragraphs naming Hobson and Hilferding; these are distinct textual components. Supports the author’s censorship explanation and monopoly/finance/imperialism framing. His inevitability, war and labor-aristocracy claims are political-theoretical arguments, not established facts. Main book chapters, original-language collation and editorial party judgments remain outside this check.

## weber_method_primary

Max Weber, methodological foundations of sociology and excerpt from “Objectivity” in social science (1904), reproduced from Sociological Writings, edited by Wolf Heydebrand (1994).

https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/ge/weber.htm

Read all available Definition of Sociology and Methodological Foundations sections 1–11, and the entire displayed Objectivity excerpt; clipped passages were reread locally. These are excerpts, not the complete original works. Supports meaning and causal evidence, ideal types, value-relevant selection without arbitrary validity, and criticism of a juristic refutation of materialist history. Particular Meyer counterfactual use is explicit. Historical racial/animal asides are not adopted; original German and edition history uncollated.

## weber_ethic_primary

Max Weber, The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism, translated by Talcott Parsons (1930), Routledge 2001 edition: translator’s preface and conclusion.

https://platypus1917.org/wp-content/uploads/Routledge-Classics-Max-Weber-The-Protestant-Ethic-and-the-Spirit-of-Capitalism-Routledge-2001.pdf

Read title/imprint/contents, complete Parsons preface xxv–xxvii and printed 119–125 (chapter V conclusion) in the 2001 reissue/electronic 2005 witness. Parsons translates the 1920 revision of the 1904–05 essay; the separate author introduction belongs to the 1920 collected religion studies, not the original essay. Weber rejects replacing one-sided materialism with one-sided spiritualism and leaves future outcomes open. Notes 97–119, other body pages, German and original essay versions uncollated.

## rostow_stages_primary

Read complete reproduced chapter 2, including the final dynamic-production-theory discussion. Colonial/national characterizations are not adopted as facts. Other chapters and original print collation remain unread.
