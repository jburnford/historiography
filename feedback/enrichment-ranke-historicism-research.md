## Readings and exact limits

- **Ranke, three selected works:** GHDI, https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/from-vormaerz-to-prussian-dominance-1815-1866/ghdi:document-358. Read all three available selections, repeating clipped output: the 1824 preface; the World History methodological selection; the 1854 lectures on epochs/progress. English witness is Roger Wines, ed./trans., *The Secret of World History* (1981), pp. 56–59, 101–104, 157–161. The German witnesses identified by GHDI are respectively the third edition (1885), volume IX part II (1888, posthumous), and the Schieder/Berding critical edition (1971). Do not treat the later witness as an uncollated original or date all World History material to 1881. Ranke combines detailed evidence with historical relations and individuality. His theological, civilizational and exclusionary claims remain attributed; his chosen Latin/Germanic scope is not universal inclusion. No whole book or original-language collation.
- **Langlois and Seignobos, *Introduction to the Study of History*:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/29637/pg29637-images.html. Read the complete authors' preface and complete Book II chapter VI, “Interpretative Criticism (Hermeneutic),” pp. 141–154. Reread the final 7,431 characters after an output loss. The English imprint is Henry Holt, 1904, G. G. Berry translation, F. York Powell preface; the authors date their preface August 1897. Original French 1898 identified by the UQAM Classiques edition page. Their criticism of Droysen/Bernheim and their credit to Fustel are their assessments, with Fustel's pedagogy mediated through a biography. Chapter VI distinguishes meaning from factual truth, language/context, literal from oblique meaning, and conjectural allusions. Only the opening two paragraphs of Book I chapter I were additionally read. No whole book or full endnote collation. Subsequently read notes 132–139 completely: specific Fustel works, quotations and criticisms of his practice are identified, but the original works and replies remain uncollated.
- **Droysen, *Outline of the Principles of History*:** https://archive.org/details/outlineofprincip01droy; 1893 E. Benjamin Andrews translation. Read the complete November 1867 author preface; opening of the third-edition preface; §§1–11, 20–24, 28–33, 35–41 and opening §42 in archive OCR. A short §6 passage and §§35–37 were reread after clipped output. §34 is partial. Source search, criticism and interpretation cooperate; remains, transmitted accounts and monuments differ; even forgery can become evidence for another question. Interpretation includes conditions and collective forces, not complete access to personal conscience. Progress/theology remain Droysen's commitments. The OCR's “1852” for the Buckle response is suspect and is not adopted. No German collation or full appended essays.
- **Mabillon/Papebroche and documentary authority:** Randolph C. Head, “Documents, Archives, and Proof around 1700,” *Historical Journal* 56.4 (2013), 909–930, DOI 10.1017/S0018246X12000477. Read publisher abstract, metadata and displayed early references. These distinguish material/textual authentication from sovereign archival public faith; references identify Mabillon 1681 and Papebroche 1675. This is a scholarly abstract, not the full article or either Latin treatise; no continuous Mabillon-to-Ranke genealogy is established.
- **Dilthey, *Introduction to the Human Sciences* (1883):** https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/ge/dilthey1.htm. Read chapters 1, 2 and 6 completely. Chapters 3–5 remain unread. The web header says Princeton University Press but does not identify translator or exact edition, so that attribution stays unresolved. Human sciences include historical, theoretical and practical propositions; understanding does not entail abandoning all generalization or natural conditions. Do not reduce his project to disembodied spirit or declare it a completed foundation.
- **Dilthey's later formation:** Rudolf Makkreel's SEP article, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dilthey/, introduction, §§1.1–1.2 and displayed passages from §2.3 (especially objective spirit, expressions, context and biography). Also read pp. 1–3 of Makkreel/Rodi's introduction to *Selected Works* III (2002), https://assets.press.princeton.edu/chapters/i7404.pdf, plus the opening of p. 4. The publisher introduction and SEP distinguish the 1910 work from later continuation drafts, and Hegel's speculative objective spirit from Dilthey's historically embodied commonality. The later original work itself has not been read here. A 2002 date is corroborated by Princeton's later volume introduction and library metadata; digital reissues are not the first edition.
- **Croce, *Theory & History of Historiography*:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/54642/pg54642-images.html. Read title/imprint, complete first-Italian-edition preface, translator note, and complete Part I chapter I, pp. 11–26. English 1921 Ainslie translation uses the second Italian edition (1919). The 1916 preface identifies component writings from 1912–1913 and German book publication in 1915; existing Italian 1917 work date is retained. Living historical questions differ from preservation of records, yet preservation has a necessary function. Croce explicitly discusses Droysen's source categories: admiration and disagreement coexist. His idealist assertions are arguments, not the atlas's own ontology. Other chapters and original editions remain unread.
- **Meinecke:** Read BBVA/University of Santiago publisher page for the 2023 Galician translation, https://www.fbbva.es/en/publicaciones/meinecke_a-xenese-do-historicismo/, including its mediated preface quotation and identification of *Die Entstehung des Historismus* (1936). This is not the original preface. Read abstract and selected author-upload passages of Troy R. E. Paddock, “Rethinking Friedrich Meinecke's Historicism,” *Rethinking History* 10.1 (2006), 95–108, DOI 10.1080/13642520500474857: opening pp. 95–96 and passages spanning pp. 100–104, plus discontinuous search extracts. His continuity/state-power criticism is attributed, not a settled verdict. No complete article or Meinecke original chapter read. The later bibliography does not extend mapped coverage.
- **Collingwood:** Brock Mead Project identifies *The Idea of History* (1946), Epilegomena §4, pp. 282–302. Direct curl and escalated retry both failed with certificate error; browser returned 502. No certificate check was bypassed. Read the complete first paragraph in the indexed Brock text. Then read primary paragraphs 1–7 and 39–45 in David Pierce's annotated transcription, https://polytropy.com/philosophy/collingwoods-history-as-re-enactment-of-past-experience/. Separate his added commentary, bullet formatting and summaries from Collingwood's text. Pierce discloses an unidentified PDF witness and checks against a 1994 Oxford paperback; this is not an independently collated critical edition. The selected argument requires evidence and rethinking, distinguishes thought from the irrecoverable immediacy of feeling, and resists both total-context and atomistic accounts. An imaginary objection invoking Croce is not Collingwood's final position. Original *Autobiography* chapter V not independently obtained; selected quotations on the annotated page are not a whole-chapter reading.
- **Skinner's reception:** https://concepta-net.org/files/Image/Skinner_Interview.pdf. Read complete answers to questions 1 and 6 in the 33-page unpaginated manuscript, including the warning that retrospective self-narration can become too tidy. Interviewers Petri Koikkalainen and Sami Syrjämäki; published as “Quentin Skinner on Encountering the Past,” *Finnish Yearbook of Political Thought* / *Redescriptions* 6 (2002), 34–63, DOI 10.7227/R.6.1.4, metadata corroborated by the journal issue. Explicit Collingwood question-and-answer reception concerns Skinner's early work. He also stresses Wittgenstein, Austin and Grice and denies Oakeshott's influence on himself. His speculation about Austin knowing Collingwood is not evidence for a new transmission arrow. No complete interview or 1969 article collation. The 2002 retrospective does not extend mapped scope.
