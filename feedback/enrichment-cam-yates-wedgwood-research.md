## Yates

- Primary scan https://www.aproged.pt/biblioteca/Yatestheartofmemory.pdf saved as `/tmp/historiography-yates-memory.pdf` (222 PDF pages). Title/copyright leaves and contents read: first published 1966; scan includes 1984 ARK and 1999 Selected Works reprint preliminaries. Complete preface xi–xv and complete chapter XVII, pp. 368–389, read. One oversized output was truncated; missing opening pp. 368–371 and pp. 372–373 were separately reread. Other substantive chapters remain unread.
- Preface separates rhetorical places/images, medieval prudence, Renaissance occult memory and the different origin/procedures of Lullism. Bing’s draft criticism, Gombrich’s help, Walker’s discussions/manuscript/translation checking, Trapp’s bibliography and R. W. Yates’s reading and drawings are explicit. Yates knew Aby Warburg through Bing, not personally. Preserve her tentative recollection that Gombrich first supplied Camillo’s book.
- Rossi’s Clavis universalis (1960) is expressly valued while described as different in approach. Chapter XVII uses him repeatedly, including qualified agreement over Bacon, and develops an argument connecting mnemonic ordering and combinatory projects with new methods. The chapter’s stronger claims about Leibniz/calculus, Bruno’s religion and secret transmission are Yates’s interpretations or hypotheses, not independently checked findings. Her conjectured Hermetic society is not an established institutional link. She explicitly calls the history incomplete.
- Ernst Gombrich, “On Frances Yates,” NYRB, 3 March 1983, complete memorial text read at https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1983/03/03/on-frances-yates/ . Participant account identifies Singer/Wind contact, Warburg visual evidence, Bing/Saxl criticism and Yates’s defended larger interpretations; it admires her insight while registering evidential hesitation. It expressly declines a full evaluation. This is not a case-by-case refutation or proof that all Warburg scholars agreed.
- Brian Vickers, ed., Occult and Scientific Mentalities in the Renaissance (1984): publisher description/contents and primary introduction opening consulted at https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/occult-scientific-mentalities/BB1B27F238D5B2D4380B35767B3985ED and https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/occult-scientific-mentalities/introduction/03618F8914301DF752116A8C0F6CE70A .
