## What the journals reveal

**Three strong candidates for dedicated atlas entries emerged.** This is an editorial assessment of the present descriptions, scope notes, approaches and people, supported by journal/field evidence. It does not mean these subjects are entirely absent.

Counts below are distinct inventory candidates with checked classifications in the named subject category. They combine library, directory and publisher evidence, include journals outside the historical period, and are not worldwide totals, prestige scores or completeness measures. Examples are independently selected catalogue titles; an example need not itself carry that category classification. The machine-readable companion lists the actual counted IDs.

### 1. Public history — strongest next addition

- **Journal signal:** *The Public Historian* (1978); six checked candidates in “Heritage and public history.” Its UCSB founding is documented by [NCPH](https://ncph.org/history-at-work/update-on-the-journal/).
- **Already present:** `oral/public_authority`, `memory/urban_landscape_practice`, `geography/urban_public_history`, and the democratic work of `historyworkshop`. Dolores Hayden’s urban/public-memory work is represented.
- **Missing account:** the formation of public history as professional practice; work in government, museums, preservation and consultancy; the relationship between commissioned questions, professional independence and public participation. No dedicated field entry or shared people records for Robert Kelley and G. Wesley Johnson.
- **Source basis:** Kelley’s first-issue essay defines public history around historical work in practical settings and describes the graduate-training initiative. Pages 16–21 were read in a [university-hosted copy](https://eclass.uowm.gr/modules/document/file.php/ELED278/Kelley%20public%20history%201978%20PH.pdf). This is one US founding formulation, not a universal definition or the start of all public uses of history.
- **Recommended entry:** a field/practice entry connecting these existing approaches. Research differing national formations and disputes over authority before writing it. Do not redirect *The Public Historian* to History Workshop simply because both engage wider publics.

### 2. History of medicine and health — strong field gap

- **Journal signal:** *Medical History*, *Bulletin of the History of Medicine*, *Social History of Medicine*; seven checked candidates in “Medicine and health.”
- **Already present:** `epistemology/concepts_norms`, `geography/human_medical_ecology`, `science/african_health_healing`, `science/human_sciences_professions`, `africanhist/health_healing`, and History Workshop’s motherhood/population-health work. Roy Porter is already a shared person; do not duplicate him.
- **Missing account:** the field’s changing relations among medical practitioners, social historians, patients, therapeutic knowledge and public health. Existing examples are substantial but dispersed; they do not explain the methodological trajectory as a field.
- **Source basis:** the society describes its [1970 origins and changing aims](https://sshm.org/values-of-the-society-for-the-social-history-of-medicine/). Its editors identify *Social History of Medicine*’s 1988 launch as successor to the Bulletin and select pre-2001 work on mortality, patient records and knowledge. [Anniversary introduction](https://academic.oup.com/shm/pages/chairs-choices-anniversary-vi).
- **Recommended entry:** a research field with distinct professional, social, patient-centred and colonial/postcolonial approaches. Review Sigerist, Ackerknecht, Rosen, Roy Porter, Dorothy Porter and the existing African-healing scholarship without suggesting one linear replacement story. The medical historian Dorothy Porter is not the existing librarian Dorothy Porter Wesley. Protect predecessor/current-title dates when adding future edges.

### 3. Urban history — strong field and methods gap

- **Journal signal:** *Journal of Urban History* (1974), *Urban History* and *Urban History Review*; eight checked candidates in “Urban.”
- **Already present:** `environment/urban_hinterland`, `geography/urban_public_history`, `memory/urban_landscape_practice`, `queer/communities`, plus economic and quantitative history. Cities occur as sites and problems, without a dedicated account of urban historiography.
- **Missing account:** urban history’s organization and methodological disagreements, including comparisons among quantitative population/mobility research, the built environment, local power and social relations. H. J. Dyos is not in the shared people registry.
- **Source basis:** the [1974 Yearbook editorial extract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/urban-history/50th-anniversary-collection) describes an expanding community, topics and techniques. Cannadine’s [1982 retrospective summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/exploring-the-urban-past/urban-history-in-the-united-kingdom-the-dyos-phenomenon-and-after-by-david-cannadine/B7CC973BFEA45D82EF63080684F7B8D9) distinguishes British organization around Dyos from US New Urban History. These are extracts, not full-article checks.
- **Recommended entry:** distinguish those approaches and their later criticism. Reconcile the Newsletter, *Urban History Yearbook* and *Urban History* title sequence before attaching a 1974 founding event to a title; *Journal of Urban History* is a different journal.

## Further leads, with weaker conclusions

| Lead | Checked candidates | Assessment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Archaeology | 37 | A substantial methodological review priority: processual/post-processual debates have no dedicated entry. Existing African historical methods include archaeology. Foucault’s “archaeology” is a different use of the word. Binford, Hodder and Childe are absent from shared people, but name absence is not sufficient evidence for a new node. Review the atlas’s disciplinary boundary and primary methodological texts first. |
| Legal history | 14 | Law already matters in Weber, political history, historical sociology and Thompson. Review whether a coherent account of legal-historical methods is missing; no new field foundation established in this pass. |
| History of education | 9 | A candidate field review, not yet a researched major-gap finding. Teaching and education references scattered through the atlas do not by themselves establish field coverage. |
| Religious history | 36 | Religion is extensively represented across existing entries. A high journal count and absent dedicated field label do not establish absent historiography. Audit institutional and methodological traditions before deciding whether to add an entry. |
| East Asia | 29 | Regional coverage review. Existing regional examples should be assessed against Chinese, Japanese, Korean and other local historiographical traditions; do not treat a region as one school. |
| Latin America and Caribbean | 30 | Review local historical traditions beyond the existing dependency, Atlantic and postcolonial entries. Counted regional subject coverage is not evidence of publication language or intellectual affiliation. |
| Islamic studies | 13 | A topic category, not a proxy for all Middle Eastern history or a single historiographical tradition. Review local traditions and languages separately. |

These are discovery leads, not claims that all important traditions are missing. The complete subject matrix is in `founding-and-gaps-v1.112.json`; it preserves the classification evidence status and sparse editorial correspondences.

## Implementation and validation

New claims are stored in the hash-pinned `venue-batches/founding-001.json`, applied **after** accepted library metadata. This avoids changing the exact prior-record fingerprints used by the immutable bibliographic imports. Only new sources, journal source references, necessary research-journal role promotion and five edges are admitted. All dates, accepted profiles, subject classifications, existing journal claims and historical graph content are preserved.

The snapshot is `drafts/historiography-1920-2000.v1.111.json`. The companion JSON records exact preservation checks and catalogue reproducibility. Journal edges are present in the dataset; the current historical-graph renderer does not yet display the separate journal graph. No deployment was performed.

Validation: 54 relevant Python tests and the JavaScript core suite passed; the graph validator reports zero errors and four unchanged historical warnings. Exact preservation and offline catalogue rebuild checks passed.
