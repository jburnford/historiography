# Journal founding programmes: expanded review, revision 1.113

Added **14 founding-programme edges**, bringing the separate journal graph to **22 founding links and one debate link**. This substantially expands the initial five-link addition. It remains a targeted historical review, not a completed assessment of all 1,628 candidate periodicals.

A founding edge records a journal created to advance or consolidate a documented research programme. It does not date the birth of an entire field. Where a journal is interdisciplinary, the qualification identifies the component that matches the atlas target.

## Evidence and interpretation

- Primary editorial readings support Comparative Studies in Society and History (opening pp. 1–3) and Environment and History (complete two-page editorial). History in Africa uses the publisher’s opening editorial extract, explicitly not the entire article.
- Feminist Studies and Signs use institutional histories; the latter reproduces the founding editorial. Their programmes encompass women’s studies more broadly than women’s history. Feminist Studies organizing in 1969 is distinguished from first publication in 1972.
- Gender & History uses an explicit scholarly chapter abstract by Antoinette Burton. The full chapter and 1989 editorial have not been read. This limited evidence supports a narrow purpose claim; it does not establish every later programme or the full racial-formation target.
- Social Science History uses an editorial retrospective quoting the founding constitution; The Economic History Review uses the society’s historical research on its first issue. The Review’s first publication is 1927, while the society was established in 1926.
- Journal of Interdisciplinary History uses a founder’s interview. Quantification was part of a wider interdisciplinary project. CSSH likewise reaches beyond historical sociology; neither link assigns the journal every theory in the target entry.
- Isis retains the existing 1912 foundation date. Holton distinguishes the founding programme dated November 1912 from volume 1 published in 1913. Sarton’s initial synthesis and subsequent journal pluralism are not conflated.
- Oral History Review uses the contemporary association launch notice. Its 1972 conference papers do not make 1972 the journal’s publication start. Nations and Nationalism uses an editorial history reproducing the 1995 aims.
- Holocaust and Genocide Studies uses G. E. Gorman’s contemporary review, read from a page image. The isolated newsletter page does not establish its own issue date. Reviewer speculation about future subjects is excluded.
- History and Anthropology uses Steven Seidenberg’s contemporary review, including the editorial aims. The scan is hosted under another review’s ORA record but includes Seidenberg’s actual pp. 262–264. The journal is distinct from the History of Anthropology series.

## Atlas gaps and next research

**Public history, medicine/health, and urban history remain the strongest dedicated-entry candidates.** Their existing partial coverage and the full 140-category subject matrix are documented in the [1.112 gap report](founding-and-gaps-v1.112.md). This revision preserves those findings; it does not claim to have repeated the entire gap audit.

Two further coverage questions emerged from matching journals to targets. **Broader intellectual history/history of ideas** should be investigated: the Cambridge School entry is a specific programme, and a local search finds no Lovejoy representation. **Comparative genocide studies** deserves a coverage check because the Holocaust and Genocide Studies founding programme extends beyond the existing Holocaust entry. These are research leads, not established omissions of all relevant content.

The accepted Environment and History programme also supports a geographical check: environmental history’s institutional story should include exchanges involving Africa and Asia alongside North American venues. Regional journal counts alone cannot identify a missing school.

## Preservation and validation

The pre-change atlas is saved as `drafts/historiography-1920-2000.v1.112.json`. Fourteen journal records gain founding references and, where needed, a research-journal role. Only two absent dates are filled: Oral History Review 1973 and History in Africa 1974. Cessation and current activity remain unknown. All pre-existing dates, identifiers, bibliographic profiles, classifications, candidate IDs, prior venue claims and historical graph content are preserved.

Current inventory: 1,628 periodical candidates; 798 reviewed bibliographic profiles; 799 with ISSNs; 557 with publication starts, including the unchanged 545 catalogued title starts. The 864 candidates with checked subjects, 442 provisional-only and 322 unclassified remain unchanged.

Reproduce with `python3 scripts/build_journal_catalogue.py` and `python3 scripts/audit_journal_founding_expansion.py`. The older founding audit intentionally targets 1.112. Exact preservation and offline reconstruction are recorded in [the audit](founding-expansion-v1.113.json); final checks are recorded in [validation results](founding-expansion-v1.113-validation.json).

Journal relationships remain in the separate dataset graph; the current historical renderer does not display them. Six allowlisted public assets are rebuilt. No deployment or OpenAlex work is part of this revision.
