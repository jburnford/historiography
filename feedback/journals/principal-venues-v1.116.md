# Principal journal venues — revision 1.116

**Added 11 principal-venue links.** The catalogue now has **38 principal links across 32 title records and 26 atlas fields**, plus nine founding and three debate links: **50 venue edges**, reaching 29 of 66 group entries. Five added title records bring the inventory to **1,637 candidates/title phases**, not 1,637 independently verified journals.

This continues the accepted sustained-role approach. Each edge has evidence, a reviewed interval and a target qualification. Existing historical content and all previous journal identities, dates, bibliography and classifications are preserved.

## Annales title history

The [publisher’s explicit chronology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/societies/annales-histoire-sciences-sociales) supports this sequence:

1. Annales d’histoire économique et sociale, 1929–1938.
2. Annales d’histoire sociale, 1939–1941.
3. Mélanges d’histoire sociale, 1942–1944.
4. Annales d’histoire sociale, 1945.
5. Annales. Économies, sociétés, civilisations, 1946–1993.
6. Annales. Histoire, sciences sociales, from 1994.

Four missing phases and five title relations are added. The original and HSS IDs remain unchanged. ESC and HSS share ISSN 0395-2649: they are named periods of a continuing publication. The HSS library record legitimately describes the combined run from 1946; its original observations are retained. The generic “Annales” candidate remains unresolved.

Some platform histories group the postwar run under the current title. Persée’s summary also contains an inconsistent 1929–1937 label; that row is not imported. Title succession follows the publisher’s explicit chronology. No wartime principal role is inferred merely from continuity.

## Evidence limits and follow-up

- **Ethnohistory:** university-hosted retrospective abstract plus society history; full corpus study unread. Its Indigenous-history link does not certify Indigenous control of research.
- **Social Studies of Science:** indexed retrospective opening, title-history check and an existing scoped Pinch–Bijker reading. The complete Sismondo editorial remains inaccessible.
- **Journal of the History of Sexuality:** indexed scholarly abstract/opening; full Nye article unread. The target is sexuality history, not uniform adherence to queer theory.
- **Geschichte und Gesellschaft:** located Raphael’s 25-year study, but did not obtain its body. Keep its existing founding edge; principal selection remains pending.
- **Radical History Review, Review (Fernand Braudel Center), History & Memory:** identities or sustained-role evidence still need work. Discovery leads are not accepted links.

## What this says about gaps

The Nigerian society journal was missing from the inventory despite its documented historical role. Adding it improves the African-historiography and anti-colonial connections. The atlas already has those fields: this was a missing journal/community link, not a missing entire field.

The strongest candidates for new atlas entries remain **public history, medical/health history and urban history**, from the earlier [sourced gap review](founding-and-gaps-v1.112.md). This pass establishes no additional major field omission. Venue coverage is still selective, especially for Indigenous-led publishing and regions/languages outside the first selections.
