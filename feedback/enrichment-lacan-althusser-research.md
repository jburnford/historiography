## Completed primary reading: Althusser, For Marx introduction

https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/althusser/1965/introduction.htm

**Complete introduction sections I–II and notes 1–4 read.** Read first part through web output, remainder and notes through locally downloaded text. Introduction explicitly dated March 1965. Collection metadata at https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/althusser/1965/index.htm identifies Pour Marx (1965), Ben Brewster English translation (1969). Generic archive page headings saying 1962/1963 are not the introduction’s date. Local `/tmp/historiography-althusser-for-marx-intro.html` and `.txt`.

The opening frames essays as dated interventions, retaining inconsistencies while acknowledging editorial changes. His account of French Communist philosophical poverty, Stalinism, intellectuals and workers is a situated argument, not a neutral history. He names Politzer, Cornu, Lenin, Gramsci and others. Do not treat the claimed absence of theoretical culture as established fact.

Section II explicitly borrows **problematic from Jacques Martin** and **epistemological break from Gaston Bachelard**. He says Marx’s own declaration about 1845 is a proposition to examine rather than proof. He nevertheless argues for a break and proposes a periodization; the distinction between conceptual change and instantaneous replacement matters. Words can persist while theoretical functions change. His closing circle of reading/theory is argued, not uncritically certified.

He values Della Volpe and Colletti’s distinction between Marx and Hegel but disputes locating the break in 1843. This is a specific internal disagreement. Note 4 names Engels’s Capital II preface as a resource; that source has not been newly read.

**Chronology caution:** the transcription lists Wages, Price and Profit among 1845–1857 transitional works. Do not import this as reliable publication chronology; the original witness requires collation. The listed phases are Althusser’s interpretation, not neutral historical period labels for Marx’s full corpus.
