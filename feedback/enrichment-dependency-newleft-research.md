### Dos Santos, 1970

Read complete article, printed pp. 231–236, including acknowledgments and references, in the seven-page PDF (last page blank): Theotonio dos Santos, “The Structure of Dependence,” American Economic Review 60(2), May 1970, 231–236.

https://globalisethis.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2009/09/dos-santos-t-structure-of-dependance1.pdf

Local `/tmp/historiography-dossantos-dependence.pdf`. Metadata independently matched RePEc publisher-fed record https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v60y1970i2p231-36.html . Read extracted text indices 0–2 then 3–6 completely. Dependence conditions internal structures as well as foreign relations; distinguishes colonial, financial-industrial and technological-industrial forms. Immediate development can have positive or negative changes, while the larger account predicts reproducing constraints and inequality. Discusses export foreign currency, balance of payments, patents/machinery, multinational investment, labor exploitation, limited markets and state policy. This is not simply no industrialization at all. Strong socialist-or-authoritarian conclusion is his prediction, not a verified necessary outcome. Historical data/calculations were not replicated.

Page 231 acknowledges University of Chile CESO project, data from Orlando Caputo/Roberto Pizarro and critical comments from Sergio Ramos. Names the Marxian imperialism tradition while proposing analysis from the dependent countries. Page 232 uses ECLA and discusses export economies; p. 236 criticizes ECLA/UNCTAD/BID policy sufficiency. This is a productive and critical relation, not identity. Specific references include Paul Baran, Pablo González Casanova, Frank, Arghiri Emmanuel, Myrdal and Nkrumah. Some reference spellings/dates require separate bibliographic checks before normalization; do not silently turn the cited Baran edition year into the first-publication date. Potential useful edge: dependency → economic theory critique of the comparative-cost/free-competition account, pp. 235–236, with a narrow target.
