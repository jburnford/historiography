## Editorial decisions

Preserve the two individual identities and original prose. Explain Wittgenstein's qualified meaning/use argument, varied language games and rule-following, and Ryle's category errors, knowing-how and meaningful-action distinctions. Add contextual selections in anthropology and philosophical debate, and a specific Wittgenstein/Austin resource context for Skinner. Ramsey and Sraffa are acknowledged resources in the preface, not presumed founders of historical methods.

Add three bounded relationships: Wittgenstein to contextual history through Skinner's explicit reception; Freud to Ryle for the limited challenge to transparent consciousness; and an undirected Wittgenstein/Ryle comparison. Similarity is insufficient to choose a transmission arrow between the latter. Avoid an invented Ryle-to-Dray, Ryle-to-Bourdieu or general Wittgenstein-to-all-cultural-history lineage.

Second-pass limits should be concrete: collate the Ryle lecture against an original/collected witness; test one Winch reception passage against Wittgenstein. Searches for Winch's 1964 “Understanding a Primitive Society” found bibliographic leads and a different Cambridge article with the same title, not a read copy of Winch's original; no claims from aggregator summaries enter the graph. A whole biography or complete philosophy corpus is not required for this atlas pass.
