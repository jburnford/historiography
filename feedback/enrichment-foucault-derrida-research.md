## Completed primary readings

- Foucault, “The Subject and Power,” Critical Inquiry 8/4 (1982), pp. 777–795: entire article and notes read from https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/philosophy/intranets/undergraduate/modules/ph348/2013-14/foucault_-_the_subject_and_power.pdf (`/tmp/historiography-foucault-subject.pdf`, indices 1–19). Subject formation connects three investigations; power, communication and capacities are differentiated but interlock. Action upon actions, freedom/resistance, institutional and wider relations, domination/exploitation/subjection, and reversible strategies are distinct. The first part was written in English; Leslie Sawyer translated the second. Dreyfus and Rabinow wrote the book for which this is an afterword, not this essay. Kant is an explicit resource; Habermas receives a specific qualification in note 1. Christian pastoral and comparative historical claims remain attributed. The temporary author blurb and planned book volumes are not reliable publication chronology.
- Foucault, “La vie: l’expérience et la science” (1985): complete AVAILABLE OPENING EXCERPT, not full article, read at https://www.generation-online.org/p/fpfoucault10.htm (`/tmp/historiography-foucault-canguilhem.html`, all 21 p elements including framing/ellipsis). Revised 1978 Canguilhem preface according to editorial introduction. Canguilhem’s role, Foucault’s selective intersecting subject/experience and concept/knowledge traditions, French epistemology/Frankfurt comparison, and the first Fontenelle point are present. Notes and original Canguilhem/Fontenelle works are not read. His map is a situated interpretation, not a universal taxonomy.
- Foucault, “Nietzsche, Genealogy, History” (1971; English collection 1977), printed pp. 139–152 and notes 1–39 read completely: https://cla.csulb.edu/departments/philosophy/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Foucault-1977-Nietzsche-Genealogy-History.pdf (`/tmp/historiography-foucault-genealogy.pdf`, indices 1–14). Sections 1–4 complete, section 5 opening only. Documentary detail, shifting uses of origin/descent/emergence, embodied history and struggles over rules oppose teleological recovery of a pure origin. The essay’s biological, religious and violence claims remain attributed interpretations of Nietzsche. Cited originals are not independently collated; pp. 153 onward unread.
- Foucault, Archaeology of Knowledge (1969; English 1972), COMPLETE three available chapters “Unities of Discourse,” “Discursive Formations,” and “Formation of Objects”: https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/fr/foucault.htm (`/tmp/historiography-foucault-archaeology.html`). Actual statements, justified constructions of unities, rules of dispersion, and relations linking institutional/social practices with objects of discourse; not a claim that everything is merely language. MIA’s generic chapter heading is not reliable internal chapter numbering. Full book and original edition not read; transcription has typos.
- Derrida, Of Grammatology, Spivak translation, CORRECTED 1997 edition: title/copyright PDF indices 2–3, complete printed pp. 27–35 and 44–50 (indices 116–124 and 133–139), including on-page notes, read from https://taradajko.org/get/books/of_grammatology.pdf (`/tmp/historiography-derrida-grammatology.pdf`). Title/copyright identify French 1967, first American 1976, corrected 1997. Copyright 1974 does not establish a first American publication in 1974. The old MIA header’s exact witness remains unidentified. Chapter opening treats Husserl’s geometry, historicity and writing; Saussure’s internal/external and speech/writing hierarchy; arbitrariness as nonnatural attachment, not private caprice. Peirce is an explicit conceptual resource, Freud an explicit interpretive reference, and Husserl/Heidegger require critical working-through. Pp. 36–43, endnotes and original French not collated. No diagram claims made.
- Derrida, “Letter to a Japanese Friend,” dated 10 July 1983, complete English transcription read at https://pdfcoffee.com/jacques-derrida-letter-to-a-japanese-friend-pdf-free.html (`/tmp/historiography-derrida-japanese.html`), reproducing an EGS page. Header identifies Derrida and Différance, Wood/Bernasconi, 1985, pp. 1–5; another catalog has conflicting pagination, so original printed edition remains uncollated. The course PDF https://grattoncourses.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/letter_to_a_japanese.pdf returned 403/internal error and was not read. Heidegger’s Destruktion/Abbau is an explicit adapted resource; deconstruction is neither a mechanical procedure nor annihilation. Social/institutional/political structures enter the account. The claim about critique has a specific Kantian context, not a prohibition on representing critical exchanges. Translation and context matter; passing names do not automatically warrant affiliations.
