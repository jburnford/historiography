## Barbara W. Tuchman

- *A Distant Mirror* (1978): [Ballantine ebook PDF](https://bosnett.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Barbara-W.-Tuchman-A-Distant-Mirror.pdf), local `/tmp/historiography-tuchman-mirror.pdf`, 790 reflowed PDF pages. Copyright 1978, eISBN 9780307793690, v3.1. Read complete acknowledgments (PDF p. 10), foreword “The Period, the Protagonist, the Hazards” and money/references notes (PDF pp. 16–24). These offsets are not original print pagination. Tuchman explicitly leaves her initial Black Death causal question unanswered; Coucy is a chosen narrative vehicle, not a representative sample. Chronology, inflated numbers, variant names, conflicting accounts, evidence gaps and negative documentary selection complicate reconstruction. Her numerical “law” is an aphorism, not an audited finding. Religion poses a difficulty for her sympathy; she admires Gibbon yet rejects irony as sufficient understanding. Modern parallels and permanent conduct are arguments to assess.
- The foreword explicitly acknowledges chroniclers, nineteenth-/early-twentieth-century editors and sociological medievalists after Marc Bloch. It does not establish a single Annales affiliation. Bloch/Huizinga note references were located, but no specific body/notes chain was fully collated for a new individual influence arrow. Do not manufacture one from a citation alone.
- Acknowledgments distinguish Alma Tuchman’s substantial research, Alma and Katrina Romney’s critical reading, Robert Gottlieb’s editing, and Elizabeth A. R. Brown/John Henneman’s specialist assistance. Many further scholars, librarians and field contacts appear. Selective shared profiles do not imply that unnamed assistance was unimportant or that advisers endorsed every conclusion.
- “In Search of History,” in *Practicing History*: [complete publisher-provided excerpt](https://penguinrandomhousehighereducation.com/book/?isbn=9780345303639) read, local `/tmp/historiography-tuchman-practicing.html` and `.txt`. Consulted paperback metadata 1982, 352 pp.; this does not newly establish first collection publication. Excerpt begins with childhood reading, discusses Radcliffe teachers Irving Babbitt, John Livingston Lowes and C. H. McIlwain, Widener research, journalism and prose, and ends after the *Guns* weather example. It is not the complete essay. Tuchman explicitly invokes Catherine Drinker Bowen’s attention to readers and Ranke’s historical truth, while acknowledging imperfect reconstruction and prohibiting invented detail. The weather claim is her example, not independently collated here. The undergraduate thesis concerns imperialists’ moral justification, not her endorsement of empire.
- [Yale finding aid](https://ead-pdfs.library.yale.edu/4053.pdf), local `/tmp/historiography-tuchman-yale.pdf`: only relevant pp. 23–24 consulted. Folder 496 records a Greenwich Library version on 27 November 1962; folder 497 lists correspondence dated 25 March 1963 and a 1963 *Radcliffe Quarterly* copy. Correspondence date is not the delivery date. Manuscripts not read. The finding aid stays a research note, not a blanket primary verification.
- Lawrence Stone, [“The Worst of Times?”](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1978/09/28/the-worst-of-times/), *New York Review of Books*, 28 September 1978: complete review read. Appreciates scholarship, narrative and public communication while criticizing analogy, Coucy as biographical focus, and selection of events at the expense of institutional/economic explanations. His uses of Geertz and Veblen are Stone’s interventions, not direct criticisms by those authors. His own historical/progress claims remain attributed. This 1978 review is not the unread 1979 *Past & Present* article.
- Elizabeth Fox-Genovese and Lawrence Stone, [“Tuchman and History”](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1978/12/07/tuchman-and-history/), 7 December 1978: complete letter and reply read. Fox-Genovese contests professional prestige as a criterion and Stone’s assumptions about crisis/progress, defending narrative’s interpretation of groups and material dislocation while explicitly saying Tuchman is not Marxist. Stone defends the distinction between synthesis and innovative research and qualifies his progress argument. Tuchman did not write this exchange. The graph retains these differences rather than declaring a winner or treating Stone as opposed to all narrative.
