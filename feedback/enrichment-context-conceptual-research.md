## Primary readings

**Skinner, “Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas,” History and Theory 8.1 (1969), 3–53**, DOI 10.2307/2504188. Successful JSTOR scan reproduction: https://dl.icdst.org/pdfs/files/38605e2c189ce8b26d3dc348bb359b1d.pdf, local `/tmp/historiography-skinner-meaning.pdf` and `.txt`, 52 PDF pages. Read title/source page and printed pp. 3–12, 35–53 completely; clipped pp. 40–41 were reread. Pages 13–34 remain unread. Opening criticizes both text-autonomy and context-as-determinant. Page 7 explicitly credits Gombrich's Art and Illusion and Kuhn's Structure; pp. 37–38 cite Wittgenstein and Collingwood; pp. 43–46 distinguish causal conditions, intended performance and illocutionary force, citing MacIntyre, Anscombe and Austin. Pages 50–51 also criticize a Collingwood inference about solved problems. Do not describe selective intellectual borrowing as discipleship. Polemical claims about Tawney, Namier, Macpherson and earlier authors are attributed, not independently certified. His final paragraph recognizes social constraints; he does not simply exclude society from meaning. Notes acknowledge Dunn and manuscript readers. A search aggregator conflated the 1969 article with a later chapter's pagination; the scanned 1969 source is authoritative here. JSTOR's web landing supplied no article text.

Entire available excerpt read, ending mid-argument; a clipped footnote on p. 11 was reread. **Dunn, “The Identity of the History of Ideas,” Philosophy 43.164 (April 1968), 85–104**, DOI 10.1017/S0031819100008986. Publisher PDF: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/6AAF16B4F05C314B32284628F3BE21F7/S0031819100008986a.pdf/the-identity-of-the-history-of-ideas.pdf; local `/tmp/historiography-dunn-identity.pdf` and `.txt`. Read pp. 85–88, 92–95, 99–101 and notes pp. 103–104; not the whole essay. Opening joins philosophical precision with historical specificity and opposes histories of abstractions acting on their own. Final acknowledgment credits discussions with Laslett and Skinner while calling the latter's account partly contrasting. Page 94 and note 20 on p. 103 explicitly use Ryle’s Plato’s Progress (1966) as a reconstruction of Plato’s intellectual activity from difficult evidence; this supports a bounded reception link. Pages 92–95 distinguish statements as acts from logical propositions and deny that social givens alone deduce a whole individual text. Publisher opening/metadata also checked.

**Koselleck, Futures Past**, Keith Tribe translation, Columbia 2004 reissue of 1985 English translation of Vergangene Zukunft (1979). https://voidnetwork.gr/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Futures-Past.-On-the-Semantics-of-historical-time-by-Reinhart-Koselleck.pdf; `/tmp/historiography-koselleck-futures.pdf` and `.txt`, 340 PDF pages. Read title/copyright/contents; complete author preface pp. 1–5; complete chapter 5, pp. 75–92; chapter 14 pp. 255–264 and 271–275. Clipped p. 261 was reread; pp. 265–270 remain unread. Chapter 5 separates word persistence from conceptual continuity, concepts as indicators and factors, synchronic/diachronic inquiry, and source vocabulary from analytical categories. It explicitly rejects identifying events with their linguistic conceptualization. Page 85 invokes Nietzsche; p. 92 discusses Weber's construction of a legitimacy category. These support specific resources. Chapter 14's formal experience/expectation pair does not independently predict actual contents. Its modernity contrast is a historical thesis with explicit simplification/unevenness and concluding recognition of recurring structures; no uniform world chronology is inferred. Original German and cited historical witnesses not collated. Collection preface says essays were edited when assembled.

**Tribe's translator introduction**, same 2004 volume, pp. x–xiv read; not complete. It distinguishes Brunner, Conze and Koselleck's programs, qualifies editorial participation and discusses Brunner's völkisch/National Socialist framing and later terminological revision through named scholarship. These are attributed scholarly findings; no original Brunner-edition collation. Its comparison with Foucault is explicitly an allusion; its disciplinary dismissal of Foucault is not adopted. No broad uniform ideological label is applied to the whole conceptual-history tradition.
