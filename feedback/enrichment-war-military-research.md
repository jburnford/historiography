## Delbrück: primary first preface and later reception

- [Public ebook sample at Legimi](https://www.legimi.de/e-book-geschichte-der-kriegskunst-im-rahmen-der-politischen-geschichte-band-1-4-hans-delbruck,b2085999.html), local `/tmp/historiography-delbruck-legimi.html` and `.txt`. Complete **“Vorrede zur ersten Auflage,” signed 4 June 1900**, read, with output gaps recovered. Complete **“Ausgangspunkt”** also read up to the heading “Erstes Buch”; this is in a later revised text (it includes a 1907 Niese reference), not all datable to the 1900 first edition. Portions of the 1908 preface were incidentally read, not the complete preface. Promotional modern description is not historical evidence. Original printing and Greek motto were not collated.
- The 1900 preface explicitly makes philological and practical criticism mutually checking parts of one method. It warns both against repeating physically impossible accounts and importing present conditions into the past. Delbrück does not claim to invent criticism or to possess a new exclusive method.
- Named resources: Rüstow’s *Geschichte der Infanterie* obtained from a regimental library in 1874; Hedwig Brühl’s mediation of the commission to complete Georg Heinrich Pertz’s Gneisenau biography in 1877; Crown Prince Friedrich’s gift of Clausewitz’s works; conversation with Friedrich, Blumenthal, Geißler and other officers; Boie’s documentary lecture notes on 1814. The commission and conversations are Delbrück’s retrospective self-account, not independently examined letters.
- He describes moving from the Gneisenau research and modern war lectures into wider chronology: 1881 lectures on 1866 and medieval military constitutions, 1883 general series, and 1897/98 lectures on economic prosperity and military organization. This supplies a specific biography-to-critical-history contribution without treating subject overlap as influence.
- **Explicit debts to Mommsen and Julius Beloch**. Beloch’s *Die Bevölkerung der griechisch-römischen Welt* (1886) supplied indispensable demographic groundwork; Delbrück both uses and corrects individual estimates, valuing their shared practical criticism. He denies having started with army-number reduction as a preconceived premise. A demography → strategic scholarship contribution is supported by this primary acknowledgment. Check the existing Beloch shared ID and roster before adding another identity.
- Delbrück closes by distinguishing his historical purpose from complete weapons/drill/naval coverage or a manual for practitioners, and explicitly says he works in **Leopold Ranke’s spirit**. This supports a specific critical-scholarship → wartradition contribution without making all military studies Ranke-derived.
- “Ausgangspunkt” explains the evidentiary choice to begin with Persian wars, not all possible earlier warfare. Its bibliography names Rüstow/Köchly’s 1852 work and 1853–55 editions, Hermann Droysen, Adolf Bauer, Hugo Liers (valued for passages but criticized for insufficient chronological/source distinctions), Niese, and Max Jähns (1889–91). These are Delbrück’s judgments, not blanket present-day endorsements. Do not confuse Hermann Droysen with Johann Gustav Droysen.
- [Uwe Walter’s complete H-Soz-Kult review](https://www.hsozkult.de/digitalreview/id/redig-3686?language=de), **26 August 2002**, read in full including all ten notes. Local `/tmp/historiography-delbruck-review.html` and `.txt`. Reviews the 2002 digital edition, with original first publication 1900–1920 and exact later editions distinguished. Documents society/economy/culture within war history, practical checks of reported numbers, strategy-of-exhaustion versus annihilation debate, and politically situated assumptions and chronological limits. Its long quoted march-column example and Caesar case remain mediated, not independently read in the original chapters.
- Walter’s presentation downplays terrain/map work relative to Kromayer/Veith; Keegan’s primary account below instead credits Delbrück’s intelligent terrain inspection. **Keep these different emphases visible; do not manufacture a uniform biography of method.** The original preface’s mutual-checking method is stronger direct evidence than either simplification.
- Walter’s note 8 explicitly quotes Delbrück declining to establish his alternative Caesar reconstruction as what actually happened: a possible account versus an impossible received one. This is not independently verified ancient history. His political interpretation and broad claim that much medieval/early-modern substance is superseded remain attributable to the review.
- `https://www.zeno.org/Geschichte/M/Delbr%C3%BCck,+Hans/Geschichte+der+Kriegskunst` timed out in browser/curl and the escalated retry; no Zeno text read. Legimi was successful. ## Clausewitz: primary selection and editorial formation

- [Graham English translation on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1946/1946-h/1946-h.htm), local `/tmp/historiography-clausewitz-primary.html`; extracted selections `/tmp/historiography-clausewitz-selections.json`. Current HTML includes all eight books despite older catalogue descriptions saying Volume 1. A catalogue title must not substitute for inspecting the file.
- **Read complete Marie von Clausewitz preface**, signed 30 June 1832; complete **Notice** (10 July 1827 plus later undated memorandum); complete **Book I chapters VI and VII** on information and friction; complete **Book II chapter III** on art/science/social life; **Book I chapter I sections 22–28 only**. Earlier sections 1–21 and remaining book are unread in this batch.
- Marie describes companionship during composition, the posthumous task, arranging/consulting on papers, Major O’Etzel’s proof/map work and her brother’s recovery/placement of the revised opening. Her modest disclaimer is a situated prefatory statement, not proof that women had no intellectual role. It warrants a shared contributor profile in strategic scholarship, not automatic membership in women’s history. Brother and O’Etzel’s full identities have not been established here; do not guess them.
- The preface names Scharnhorst’s formative guidance and records Clausewitz’s own early stylistic attraction to Montesquieu. The Notice explicitly distinguishes overthrow from limited acquisition and planned revision of political context; the unfinished work should not be treated as a perfectly uniform finished system.
- Information/friction chapters describe contradictory reports, practical impediments and the gap between plans and execution. II.3 treats war as conflict with living, reacting opponents in social life, not a mechanically applicable art/science. **The editorial footnote predicting inevitable great-power war and disparaging Hague conferences is signed EDITOR and is not Clausewitz.** The Graham/Maude apparatus must remain distinct from authorial text.
- I.1 §§22–28 combine political purpose with passions, chance and judgment and warn against arbitrarily fixing the relation of the three tendencies. This provides direct support against a wholly rational-control caricature. No claim of universally successful strategic guidance or a modern mathematical model is made.

- Includes complete acknowledgments signed **10 December 1974**, opening “A Little Learning,” “The Usefulness of Military History,” “The Deficiencies of Military History,” and the available beginning of “The ‘Battle Piece’.” Page listing is consulted 1983 edition; **1976 original** independently supported by [WorldCat record 2137107](https://search.worldcat.org/title/The-face-of-battle/oclc/2137107) and Bassford’s contemporary essay. Publication date and acknowledgment date differ.
- Keegan foregrounds his lack of combat experience, Sandhurst teaching, cadets’ anxieties, and the tension between professional training in standard procedures and academic cultivation of multiple perspectives. His restricted definition of battle excludes much colonial/guerrilla experience; this is an important **scope choice**, not proof that those wars lacked violence or historical importance. National comparisons, claims about early soldiers’ literacy and idealization of military society remain attributed, not independently accepted facts.
- He differentiates leadership, weapons, naval, institutional and doctrinal histories, then argues for renewed attention to what happens in combat. His argument that armies are for battle is his own prioritization; the graph should preserve wider military-history objects. He values memoirs but rejects a mere anthology of unexamined voices; self-interest, selective memory, documents, numbers and physical traces must be assessed together.
- **Direct resources:** Delbrück and A. H. Burne’s *Inherent Military Probability*, with a warning against uncritical timeless-army analogy; Duffy’s practical drill experiment recounted to Keegan; broader soldier contact. In the acknowledgments, academic colleagues Duffy, Richard Holmes, Gwynne Dyer, John Sweetman, David Chandler, Peter Vigor and William McElwee; Michael Howard and others; medical and personnel researchers; librarian Alan Shepperd and interlibrary-loan worker R. W. Meadows; Valerie Horsfield’s typing; Susanne Keegan’s own writing and domestic work. Selective profiles must not erase unnamed contributors.
- Keegan analyzes Napier’s Albuera rhetoric, collective images, uniform behavior and disappearing casualties while recognizing the earlier work’s achievement. He quotes Peter Young, Chandler and Howard as different narrative examples and asks how the physical actions could happen. This is method critique, not a claim that no described event occurred. Howard grants permission and contributes advice while being an object of methodological assessment; collaboration does not equal agreement.
- The [publisher’s *A History of Warfare* description](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/90840/a-history-of-warfare-by-john-keegan/) supports cultural scope only (consulted 1994 paperback). Bassford’s note 8 establishes **Knopf 1993 original**. No primary chapter from that different book has been read. Do not let the *Face* excerpt verify all its arguments.
