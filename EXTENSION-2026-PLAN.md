## Source strategy: Wikipedia plus scholarly evidence

### Shared LOD/editorial workflow and balanced coverage

Use the imported [ontology contract](ontology/README.md) as the shared research model. LOD, Wikipedia, scholarly surveys and local collections feed one review process: resolve people and works, distinguish publications/versions, then propose scoped claims and select a balanced teaching view. The [gender integration pilot](data/extension-2026/gender-integration/REPORT.md) exercises this workflow. Its crosswalk preserves the older packets and records unresolved witness/publication and claim-predicate mappings; it does not certify a complete migration.

The specialized capitalism collection has unusually deep coverage. Keep this detail available in staging without letting source counts, catalogue yield, node count or degree determine importance or overview prominence. Prioritize less-researched fields in the next breadth pass; comparable editorial attention does not require identical node quotas. Gender history must not appear secondary merely because economic history was mined more thoroughly. Evaluate geographic/language gaps as well as dates.

The LOD pilot's recent filter covers 2020–2026. The extension needs 2001–2019 too: reuse its all-period bibliographic export before broadening retrieval. Recent editions and translations do not necessarily represent new works or newly formed fields.

Wikipedia is useful for orientation, aliases, biographies, named works, debate vocabulary and finding references. Its own [verifiability policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability) requires published support and distinguishes that from original research. Article presence and popularity do not establish historiographical significance.

| Task | Starting evidence | Acceptance requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find people, works and related subjects | Wikipedia articles in relevant languages; existing Wikidata export | Record a lead, its language, article revision and retrieval date |
| Resolve identities and publication metadata | Wikidata, publishers, university profiles, library authorities, DOI records | Explicit identity match; separate work from edition/translation |
| Identify developments within a field | Historiographical review essays, handbooks, scholarly introductions and special issues | Identify the argument, scope and date; retain disagreement and regional limits |
| Establish a critique, borrowing or contribution | The relevant passage in a work or explicit scholarly account | A citation attached to that specific claim, with location and access limitations |
| Establish wider reception or influence | Reception studies and independent scholarly discussion | Evidence of reception; an author's assertion alone does not establish influence |
| Describe a very recent development | Dated publications and disciplinary documentation | Mark emerging or disputed interpretations; do not invent settled importance |

Follow Wikipedia references to their sources; record Wikipedia-only assertions as provisional. Search field/debate/work pages as well as biographies. Do not turn hyperlinks, categories, co-mentions, citations or common employment into historical influence edges. Multiple language editions repeating one source are not independent corroboration. A recent page edit is not evidence of a recent scholarly development.

Use the journal catalogue to find suitable venues and review essays. Preserve the distinction between venue relevance and an accepted principal/founding/debate role. Supplement weak Wikipedia coverage with regional scholarly surveys, university profiles and relevant-language bibliographies.

### 4. Extend in thematic batches

Choose batches using both ledgers. Every candidate in the [field-discovery queue](FIELD-DISCOVERY-2026.md) requires an explicit disposition; it cannot be silently absorbed into these broad themes. Initial search priorities, **not already verified additions**, are:

- Global, connected and economic history: divergence, capitalism, empire and challenges to global frameworks.
- Environmental history: climate, planetary scales, energy, animals and multispecies approaches. Sivasundaram's 2024 [“The Global and the Earthy”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/rethinking-global-history/global-and-the-earthy/AF01B224FEAA5B7D204D91D1BA940567) is a concrete scholarly entry point connecting global and environmental questions.
- Indigenous, postcolonial and anti-colonial histories: sovereignty, archives, decolonization and who controls historical knowledge, preserving distinctions between these programmes.
- Gender, sexuality, race, labour and social history: assess continuing arguments and methodological changes across existing entries.
- Public history, memory and Holocaust historiography: museums, commemoration, public controversies and digital practice. Include Arendt's requested candidacy and later reception, keeping earlier contributions dated to their own periods.
- Cultural, intellectual and other established histories: investigate proposed affective, sensory, material, spatial and temporal approaches without assuming that every named “turn” deserves a new field node.

Review the remaining existing topics systematically, including military, political, medical, urban and quantitative history. Search relevant regional and original-language scholarship in every batch. Newly visible people can fill older gaps as well as contribute to the post-2000 extension; label which purpose an addition serves.

Deliverable per batch: coverage-ledger updates, evidence packets, additions/corrections manifest, changed graph view, unresolved questions and validation results.

### 5. Use LLM review to make candidate discovery tractable

Start with the 42 priority and 29 further substantive candidates already reviewed, then retrieve targeted candidates for the field being researched. Do not process all 108,148 date-screened candidates as equally relevant or treat the remaining 108,064 as reviewed.

For each candidate, require: identity; role; named historical contribution; work and intervention date; candidate field/strand; proposed relation; source passage; alternative interpretation; and disposition with reason. Retain conceptual contributors such as Arendt. Churchill and Wells require assessment of their historical writing rather than automatic acceptance or exclusion by occupation.

Use the existing four prominence signals only to order research within an editorial relevance band. Wikipedia availability is useful for research access, never an admission requirement. The pre-1940 death exclusion remains a discovery default, not a rule against earlier thinkers with later reception or a reason to delete existing records.

LLMs may retrieve, extract and propose. Deterministic checks validate IDs, dates and citation locations; editorial review validates the historical interpretation. An LLM confidence score is not passage verification. Calibrate the rubric against the reviewed mixed cases before scaling it.

### 6. Publish a dated, auditable extension

Separate origins, publications, scholarly activity, lifespan, reception and atlas coverage. A source published in 2023 may describe a 1970s development; it does not automatically supply a 2023 edge. A field's continuation to 2026 does not imply that each person remained alive or every venue role continued unchanged.

Move coverage limits into structured data and derive display labels. Keep a 2000 baseline and a 2026 view, with a change view showing the extension. Verify Annales continuity, military history's earlier roots, posthumous reception, strand endpoints, journal title transitions, and axis bounds without clipping 2026 or implying that an unfinished year is fully covered. Keep research captures outside browser assets.

Completion means all 75 current topics have an explicit review outcome, every user-supplied field seed has a documented disposition, and an independent discovery pass has sought formations beyond the initial list. All accepted new interpretive relationships need scoped evidence; new-topic and deferral decisions must be explained, unresolved gaps remain visible, and historical IDs/snapshots remain intact. It does not mean exhaustive coverage of all historians or elimination of inherited evidence debt. Report existing-topic review, new-field discovery/dispositions and supported-claim coverage rather than raw node growth.

After the initial extension, use an annual scholarly review and a lighter current-year watchlist. Reassess emerging claims as reception develops; preserve earlier interpretations when superseding them.
