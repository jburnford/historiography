# Representation in discovery, selection and graph connections

User direction, 18 September 2026: independently prompted Astra and Fable 5.1 lists of the fifty most important historians since 1950 contained seven and six women respectively (14% and 12%, user-reported; lists not supplied or independently audited). These observations warrant an audit of this project's selection process. They do not establish the composition of either training corpus or an appropriate population benchmark.

An LLM-generated canon is a discovery input, never the baseline against which missing people must justify inclusion. Wikipedia visibility, citation totals, catalogue holdings and repeated survey appearances are also observations of visibility. None alone establishes historical importance. LLM review can reproduce exclusions rather than correct them.

For every field, examine representation at three stages: discovered candidates, selected people/works, and accepted graph connections. Inspect exclusions and deferrals as well as additions. Count distinct people separately from authorship occurrences; distinguish full nodes from roster-only appearances. Audit whose contributions receive substantive relationships, including critique and methodological contribution, rather than merely mentions. Women must be considered throughout economic, political, intellectual, digital and other histories, not concentrated in gender history as a substitute for inclusion elsewhere.

Use field surveys and reception studies to recover overlooked contributions. Seek surveys with different geographic, linguistic, institutional and intellectual perspectives; two surveys repeating the same canon are not independent correction. Include relevant bibliographies and scholarly reassessments of canon formation. Record the concrete work, contribution, supporting source, and inclusion/deferment reason. A source naming a person supplies a lead, not automatic authority to create an influence edge.

Do not infer gender from names, photographs or subject specialisms. Retain unknown/not researched values; document any demographic attribute with a suitable public source and its scope. Do not force nonbinary identities into a binary tally or treat disputed mappings as established people. This audit has not yet established demographic counts for the graph or new batch.

Report denominators, unknowns, and authority-match uncertainty. Compare fields and selection stages rather than imposing an unsupported universal ratio. Investigate large imbalances with targeted source searches and explain the result. Do not silently use either an inherited imbalance or numerical parity as evidence of scholarly importance. Geography, language, race/colonial exclusions and career generation need analogous source-aware examination without inferring personal attributes.

Before a batch is proposed for production, its representation audit must have named evidence, recorded omissions investigated, and an explicit unresolved-gaps account. An empty checklist or an LLM's assurance is insufficient. This is an editorial review requirement; demographic balance and historical validity remain separate questions. The current breadth batch records this audit as pending and blocks production readiness.
